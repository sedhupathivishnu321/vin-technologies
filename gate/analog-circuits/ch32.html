Read this first: what this chapter is and is not

This chapter is a study map, not a reproduction of past papers. It does not quote, number or attribute any specific question to any specific year. The descriptions below summarise, in general terms, the kinds of Analog Circuits questions GATE ECE has asked in successive eras, and how the exam format has changed. They are an editorial synthesis intended to guide practice; they may be incomplete. To study the real questions, download the official question papers and answer keys for every year from the GATE organising institute’s website and use Chapter 31 (topic-wise archetypes) and this chapter (era-wise trends) to organise them.

32.1 Recent GATE questions

How to use recent papers effectively:

  1. Take the last 5–8 official papers. Extract every question that belongs to the Analog Circuits syllabus (Chapters 1–28 of this book).
  2. Tag each question with a topic (for example “op-amp / integrator”, “BJT / CE small-signal”) and a difficulty type (1-mark MCQ, 1-mark NAT, 2-mark numerical, 2-mark conceptual, MSQ).
  3. Solve each question twice: once with the full derivation, once with the shortcut method that would save time in the exam.
  4. Record the time taken; a 1-mark question should take about \(1.5\)–\(2\) minutes and a 2-mark about \(3\)–\(4\) minutes on average.

32.2 2020–2026 analysis

General characteristics of this era (as a study guide):

  • Question formats: a mix of MCQ, multiple-select (MSQ, introduced from GATE 2021) and numerical-answer type (NAT). MSQ has no negative marking and demands that all correct options be chosen: conceptual accuracy matters more than calculation.
  • Typical themes: MOSFET biasing and small-signal gain (including channel-length modulation and body effect), differential and cascode ideas, op-amp circuits with finite parameters (GBW, slew rate, offset), comparators and Schmitt triggers with waveform output, active filters (Sallen–Key, MFB), feedback topology identification and gain calculation, oscillator frequency and Barkhausen conditions, phase margin from Bode data.
  • Style: circuits that combine two concepts (for example a diode network followed by an op-amp), fewer pure “plug into a formula” questions, and more “choose all correct statements” questions.

Preparation focus: MOSFET small-signal analysis, op-amp non-idealities, and the logic of feedback and stability; practise MSQs by writing a one-line justification for every option.

32.3 2015–2019 analysis

  • Format: MCQ and NAT; multiple sessions per year in some years, so several distinct sets of questions are available for practice.
  • Typical themes: diode circuits with two or more diodes (state determination), clippers and clampers, BJT and MOSFET DC biasing (with \(\beta\) or \(k_n\) given), small-signal gain and input/output resistance of CE, CS and follower stages, frequency response with the Miller effect, current mirrors, differential amplifiers (CMRR), op-amp integrators and differentiators, Schmitt trigger thresholds, RC and LC oscillators, feedback amplifier gain and impedances.
  • Style: moderately long numerical problems in which the correct approximation (which element to neglect) saves most of the time.

32.4 2010–2014 analysis

  • Format: MCQ dominant, with the NAT format entering in the later part of this era.
  • Typical themes: BJT hybrid-\(\pi\) and h-parameter amplifiers, transistor biasing with stability factor, feedback topology and \(A_f\), op-amp linear applications, active filter identification (LPF/HPF/BPF from the circuit or the transfer function), waveform-output questions for clippers and rectifiers, oscillator frequency.
  • Style: concept-plus-single-calculation questions; formula recall and identification of circuit function were frequently sufficient.

32.5 2005–2009 analysis

  • Typical themes: diode and rectifier numericals, BJT DC analysis, small-signal parameters, two-stage amplifiers, op-amp basic amplifiers, feedback, and oscillator circuits.
  • Style: linked or common-data multi-part questions were used in this period and have become rare since.

32.6 Older conceptual questions

Some concepts have stayed constant across eras and are worth mastering because they recur in modified form:

  • Effect of temperature on diode/BJT quantities (leakage doubles per \(10^\circ\)C; \(V_{BE}\) falls \(2\) mV/\({}^\circ\)C).
  • Region of operation of BJT and MOSFET given node voltages.
  • Which configuration (CE/CB/CC or CS/CG/CD) has the highest/lowest input/output resistance or gain.
  • Effect of negative feedback on gain, bandwidth, distortion and impedances.
  • Slew-rate-limited output of an op-amp for a large high-frequency sine.
  • Condition of sustained oscillation (Barkhausen) and the frequency of common oscillators.

General exam format to keep in mind: 65 questions, 100 marks, 3 hours; 1-mark and 2-mark questions; negative marking for MCQ only (one-third of the marks of a 1-mark question, two-thirds for a 2-mark question); no negative marking for MSQ and NAT. Always check the latest official information brochure for the current pattern, marks weightage and syllabus.

Time plan for the Analog Circuits portion: the subject typically contributes roughly a tenth of the paper (about \(8\)–\(14\) marks, varying by year and set); a reasonable budget is \(15\)–\(25\) minutes in total if you are fast, ideally leaving detailed circuits for the second pass.
