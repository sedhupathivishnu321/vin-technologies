Preface: How to Use This Book

chapterPreface: How to Use This Book

This book covers the Analog Circuits portion of the GATE Electronics & Communication Engineering syllabus. It runs from device fundamentals (diodes, BJTs, MOSFETs) through amplifier analysis, current mirrors, differential pairs, operational amplifiers, filters, oscillators, feedback, and stability. Every chapter has the same anatomy.

  • Concept and derivation. Formulas are derived once, so you can rebuild them under exam pressure instead of memorising them.
  • Figures for each case. Each circuit variant (for example a series and a shunt clipper, positive and negative) has its own schematic and its own input–output waveform.
  • Solved examples (orange boxes) with complete numerical working.
  • GATE shortcuts (green boxes) and conceptual traps (red boxes), the two things that decide marks in a 3-hour paper.
  • GATE-pattern problems (violet boxes) followed by answers, and practice problems with answers at the end of each chapter.
Important note on the “GATE previous-year” problems

The problems labelled GATE-pattern are original problems written to match the style, difficulty and recurring ideas of past GATE ECE papers. They are not verbatim copies of past questions, and this book does not attach a year or question number to any of them. Chapters 31 and 32 give a topic-wise and era-wise analysis of what GATE repeatedly tests, written as a study map, not as a reproduction of the papers. For the exact official questions, download the papers from the GATE organising institute’s website and use this book as the solution toolkit.

Conventions. Unless stated otherwise: \(V_T=25.85\) mV (about \(26\) mV) at \(300\) K; a silicon diode has \(V_{on}=0.7\) V; BJTs are silicon with \(V_{BE}=0.7\) V; MOSFET current uses \(k_n'=\mu_nC_{ox}\) with \(I_D=\tfrac12k_n'\tfrac WL(V_{GS}-V_{th})^2\) in saturation; op-amps are ideal unless a finite parameter is stated; small-signal quantities are lower-case (\(v_{be}\)), total instantaneous quantities are lower-case with upper-case subscripts (\(v_{BE}\)), and DC quantities are upper-case (\(V_{BE}\)).

A suggested way to study. Read the derivation once, then close the book and re-derive the result on paper. Do the solved examples covering the solution first. Attempt the GATE-pattern problems under a timer of roughly two minutes per mark. Use the Formula Book (Chapter 30) and the one-page sheets (Appendix C) in the last week.
