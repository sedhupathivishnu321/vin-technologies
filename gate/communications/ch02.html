Noise, SNR and the Matched Filter

GATE Focus

Thermal noise power (\(kTB\)), white and Gaussian noise models, narrowband noise components and the Rayleigh envelope, signal-to-noise ratio, noise figure and the cascade (Friis) formula, and the matched filter with its \(2E/N_0\) output SNR.

2.1 Sources of noise

  • Thermal (Johnson) noise: random motion of electrons in a resistor; Gaussian, white up to very high frequencies.
  • Shot noise: discreteness of charge crossing a junction; \(\overline{i_n^2}=2qIB\).
  • Flicker (\(1/f\)) noise: dominant at low frequency. Partition and quantisation noise in other blocks. External: atmospheric, man-made, cosmic.
Thermal noise

Open-circuit rms voltage: \(\overline{v_n^2}=4kTRB\) (\(k=1.38\times10^{-23}\) J/K).
Maximum available noise power: \(P_n=kTB\) (into a matched load). One-sided PSD \(N_0=kT\); two-sided \(N_0/2\).
At \(T_0=290\) K: \(N_0=kT_0=4.0\times10^{-21}\) W/Hz \(=-174\) dBm/Hz.   \(-174\,\text{dBm}+10\log_{10}B\) gives the noise floor.

Solved Example

\(R=1\,\)k\(\Omega\), \(B=1\) MHz, \(T=290\) K: \(v_{n,rms}=\sqrt{4(1.38\times10^{-23})(290)(10^3)(10^6)}=4.0\,\mu\)V. Available noise power in \(1\) MHz: \(kTB=4\times10^{-15}\) W \(=-114\) dBm.

2.2 White Gaussian noise (AWGN)

Idealised noise with a flat PSD \(S_n(f)=N_0/2\), \(R_n(\tau)=\frac{N_0}2\delta(\tau)\), with Gaussian amplitude statistics. Samples at distinct times are uncorrelated and therefore independent. After an ideal filter the noise power is finite: low-pass of bandwidth \(B\) (passband \(-B\) to \(B\)) gives \(\sigma^2=\dfrac{N_0}2\cdot2B=N_0B\); band-pass of bandwidth \(B_T\) (positive frequencies) around \(\pm f_c\) gives \(\sigma^2=\dfrac{N_0}2\cdot2B_T=N_0B_T\). The output of a correlator with a unit-energy reference signal has variance \(N_0/2\) (used in detection, Ch. 8).

2.2.1 Narrowband (band-pass) noise

Quadrature representation

\(n(t)=n_c(t)\cos2\pi f_ct-n_s(t)\sin2\pi f_ct\).
\(n_c\) and \(n_s\) are independent zero-mean Gaussian low-pass processes with equal variance equal to the total power of \(n(t)\): \(\sigma_c^2=\sigma_s^2=\sigma^2=\E[n^2]\).
Envelope \(r=\sqrt{n_c^2+n_s^2}\) is Rayleigh: \(f_R(r)=\dfrac r{\sigma^2}e^{-r^2/2\sigma^2}\), mean \(\sigma\sqrt{\pi/2}\); phase is uniform in \((0,2\pi)\).
Signal plus noise \(A\cos2\pi f_ct+n(t)\): envelope is Rician.

For an ideal band-pass PSD \(N_0/2\) of bandwidth \(B_T\) around \(\pm f_c\): \(\sigma^2=N_0B_T\). In an AM/DSB receiver with \(B_T=2W\) this is \(2N_0W\).

2.3 Signal-to-noise ratio

\(\text{SNR}=P_s/P_n\) (power ratio); in dB \(=10\log_{10}(P_s/P_n)\). For signals expressed in voltage ratios use \(20\log_{10}\). With energy per bit: \(\dfrac{E_b}{N_0}=\text{SNR}\cdot\dfrac{B}{R_b}\) (SNR \(=E_bR_b/(N_0B)\)).

2.4 Noise figure and noise temperature

Noise figure

\(F=\dfrac{\text{SNR}_{in}}{\text{SNR}_{out}}\ge1\), \(\ NF=10\log_{10}F\). Equivalent input noise temperature \(T_e=(F-1)T_0\), \(T_0=290\) K; \(F=1+T_e/T_0\).
Output noise power of a device of gain \(G\): \(N_{out}=GkB(T_s+T_e)\).
Friis cascade: \(F=F_1+\dfrac{F_2-1}{G_1}+\dfrac{F_3-1}{G_1G_2}+\cdots\)   and   \(T_e=T_1+\dfrac{T_2}{G_1}+\dfrac{T_3}{G_1G_2}+\cdots\) (linear ratios).
Attenuator (matched, loss \(L=1/G\)): \(F=L\), \(T_e=(L-1)T_0\).
System sensitivity: \(P_{min}\,(\text{dBm})=-174+10\log_{10}B+NF+\text{SNR}_{min}(\text{dB})\).

Receiver chain

LNA: \(F_1=2\) dB \(=1.585\), \(G_1=20\) dB \(=100\); mixer/IF: \(F_2=10\) dB \(=10\). Overall \(F=1.585+\dfrac{10-1}{100}=1.675\) \(\Rightarrow\) \(2.24\) dB. The LNA gain makes the second stage almost irrelevant.
\(F=2\) dB: \(T_e=(1.585-1)\times290=170\) K.
Sensitivity for \(B=1\) MHz, \(NF=5\) dB, required SNR \(10\) dB: \(-174+60+5+10=-99\) dBm.

2.5 Matched filter

Problem: choose the receiver filter \(h(t)\) that maximises the SNR at the sampling instant \(T\) for a known pulse \(s(t)\) in white Gaussian noise of PSD \(N_0/2\).

Matched filter

\(h(t)=s(T-t)\) (time-reversed, delayed copy of \(s\)); \(\ H(f)=S^*(f)e^{-j2\pi fT}\).
Maximum output SNR: \(\left(\dfrac SN\right)_{max}=\dfrac{2E}{N_0}\), \(E=\displaystyle\int_0^Ts^2(t)dt\) — depends only on the pulse energy, not on its shape.
Output at \(t=T\): \(y(T)=E\); noise variance \(\dfrac{N_0}2E\). Equivalent to a correlator: \(y=\int_0^Tr(t)s(t)dt\) (both give the same sample at \(t=T\)).
For coloured noise, whiten first: \(H(f)=\dfrac{S^*(f)}{S_n(f)}e^{-j2\pi fT}\).

Solved Example

Rectangular pulse of amplitude \(2\) V and duration \(1\,\mu\)s: \(E=A^2T=4\times10^{-6}\) J. With \(N_0=10^{-9}\) W/Hz: \(\text{SNR}_{max}=2E/N_0=8000\) \((39\,\text{dB})\). The matched filter for a rectangle is an integrate-and-dump circuit with \(h(t)=A\) for \(0\le t\le T\).

Conceptual Trap

(i) \(N_0/2\) is two-sided; \(kT\) is the one-sided \(N_0\). (ii) Noise figure is a power ratio \(\ge1\); in dB \(\ge0\). (iii) Friis uses linear gains and \(F\). (iv) The matched-filter SNR depends on pulse energy only; doubling the amplitude quadruples \(E\). (v) The Rayleigh envelope has non-zero mean even though \(n_c,n_s\) have zero mean.

GATE Shortcut

Noise floor: \(-174+10\log B\) dBm. Cascade: first-stage \(F\) and gain dominate. Matched-filter SNR: \(2E/N_0\); if the pulse is \(\pm A\) over \(T\) and \(E_b=A^2T\).

2.6 GATE problems

GATE 1-Mark Problems

Q1. The thermal noise PSD at \(T_0=290\) K is (A) \(4\times10^{-21}\) W/Hz (B) \(4\times10^{-18}\) W/Hz (C) \(4\times10^{-24}\) W/Hz (D) \(1.38\times10^{-23}\) W/Hz.

Q2. The envelope of narrowband Gaussian noise has a (A) Gaussian (B) Rayleigh (C) uniform (D) Rician pdf.

Q3. The maximum output SNR of a matched filter is (A) \(E/N_0\) (B) \(2E/N_0\) (C) \(E^2/N_0\) (D) \(4E/N_0\).

Q4. An attenuator of 3 dB at 290 K has a noise figure of (A) 0 dB (B) 1.5 dB (C) 3 dB (D) 6 dB.

Answers: Q1 (A). Q2 (B). Q3 (B). Q4 (C).

GATE 2-Mark Problems

Q5. LNA \(F_1=2\) dB, \(G_1=20\) dB followed by a stage with \(F_2=10\) dB. The overall noise figure is (A) 2.24 dB (B) 12 dB (C) 3 dB (D) 10 dB.

Q6. A resistor of \(1\,\)k\(\Omega\) at 290 K with 1 MHz bandwidth has rms thermal noise voltage (A) 4 \(\mu\)V (B) 40 \(\mu\)V (C) 0.4 \(\mu\)V (D) 4 mV.

Q7. A rectangular pulse of amplitude 2 V and duration \(1\,\mu\)s in white noise with \(N_0=10^{-9}\) W/Hz. The matched-filter peak SNR in dB is (A) 36 (B) 39 (C) 42 (D) 33.

Answers: Q5 (A). Q6 (A). Q7 (B): \(8000\).

Numerical Answer Type (NAT)

Q8. Noise temperature (K, nearest integer) of an amplifier with \(NF=2\) dB: .

Q9. Receiver sensitivity (dBm): \(B=1\) MHz, \(NF=5\) dB, required SNR 10 dB: .

Answers: Q8: 170. Q9: \(-99\).

Multiple Select (MSQ)

Q10. Select the true statements. (A) Thermal noise is Gaussian. (B) Noise figure can be less than 1. (C) The matched filter output SNR depends only on \(E/N_0\). (D) In a cascade the first stage dominates \(F\) if its gain is large.

Answers: Q10: (A), (C), (D).

Chapter Test

T1. \(-174\) dBm/Hz means?   T2. Impulse response of the matched filter for \(s(t)\) on \([0,T]\).   T3. \(T_e\) of a noiseless device.

Answers: T1: \(kT_0\) in dBm. T2: \(s(T-t)\). T3: 0 K (\(F=1\)).
