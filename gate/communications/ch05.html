Chapter 5

Noise in Analog Modulation and Efficiency Comparison

In this chapter

  2. 5.2 Linear modulation
  3. 5.3 Frequency modulation
  4. 5.4 Comparison of analog schemes
  5. 5.5 GATE problems
GATE Focus

Figure of merit (FOM) of DSB-SC, SSB, AM and FM receivers, the effect of \(\beta\) on FM output SNR, threshold effect, pre-/de-emphasis, and the bandwidth–power trade-off between schemes.

5.1 Reference: baseband SNR

Let the message have power \(P\) and bandwidth \(W\), and the channel add white noise of PSD \(N_0/2\). The reference SNR is that of a baseband system with the same transmitted power: \[ \text{SNR}_{base}=\frac{P}{N_0W}. \] The channel SNR \(\text{SNR}_C=\dfrac{\text{average transmitted (received) power}}{N_0W}\) and the figure of merit \(\text{FOM}=\dfrac{\text{SNR}_O}{\text{SNR}_C}\) compares each scheme with this baseband benchmark.

5.2 Linear modulation

Output SNR with coherent detection

DSB-SC: \(\text{SNR}_O=\dfrac{A_c^2P}{2N_0W}=\text{SNR}_C\), FOM \(=1\).
SSB: \(\text{SNR}_O=\dfrac{A_c^2P}{4N_0W}=\text{SNR}_C\), FOM \(=1\) (half the noise bandwidth compensates the half signal power).
AM with envelope detector (large SNR): \(\text{SNR}_O=\dfrac{A_c^2k_a^2P}{2N_0W}\), \(\ \text{SNR}_C=\dfrac{A_c^2(1+k_a^2P)}{2N_0W}\), \[ \text{FOM}=\frac{k_a^2P}{1+k_a^2P}=\eta\ \ (\text{the power efficiency}),\ \ \text{tone: }\frac{\mu^2}{2+\mu^2}\ (\text{max }\tfrac13). \]

So AM is always inferior to DSB-SC/SSB in noise performance by the factor \(\eta\) (the carrier wastes power). Envelope detection has a threshold: below a carrier-to-noise ratio of about \(10\) dB the output SNR falls rapidly (noise dominates the envelope and the message is lost), whereas coherent detection of AM has no threshold.

5.3 Frequency modulation

Above threshold (large carrier-to-noise ratio) with a limiter and discriminator:

FM receiver

\(\text{SNR}_O=\dfrac{3A_c^2k_f^2P}{2N_0W^3}\), \(\ \text{SNR}_C=\dfrac{A_c^2}{2N_0W}\), hence \(\ \text{FOM}=\dfrac{3k_f^2P}{W^2}\).
Tone modulation (\(P=A_m^2/2\), \(f_m=W\), \(\beta=k_fA_m/W\)): \(\ \text{FOM}=\dfrac32\beta^2\).
PM (tone): FOM \(=k_p^2P=\dfrac{\beta^2}2\), with \(\beta=k_pA_m\).
Discriminator output noise PSD is parabolic: \(S_{n_o}(f)\propto f^2\), so high message frequencies suffer most.

FM versus AM

\(\beta=5\) (commercial FM): FOM \(=\tfrac32\times25=37.5\) (\(15.7\) dB). Tone AM with \(\mu=1\): FOM \(=\tfrac13\) (\(-4.8\) dB). FM thus gains \(15.7+4.8=20.5\) dB over AM at the same channel SNR (a factor \(112.5\)).
For \(\text{SNR}_C=40\) dB: DSB-SC/SSB: \(40\) dB; AM (\(\mu=1\)): \(35.2\) dB; FM (\(\beta=5\)): \(55.7\) dB.
AM with \(\mu=0.5\): FOM \(=1/9\) (\(-9.5\) dB); with \(\text{SNR}_C=30\) dB output SNR \(=20.5\) dB.

Bandwidth–SNR exchange. \(\text{SNR}_O\propto\beta^2\text{SNR}_C\) while \(B_T\approx2(\beta+1)W\): doubling \(\beta\) from 5 to 10 quadruples the output SNR (\(+6\) dB) but increases the bandwidth from \(180\) to \(330\) kHz (\(W=15\) kHz). This trade-off, absent in AM, is the key advantage of wideband FM; it only holds above threshold.

5.3.1 FM threshold effect

When the carrier-to-noise ratio \(\rho=\dfrac{A_c^2}{2N_0B_T}\) drops below \(\approx10\) dB, noise spikes (clicks) appear in the discriminator output and \(\text{SNR}_O\) collapses faster than linearly. The threshold rises with \(\beta\) (a wider bandwidth admits more noise). Threshold extension: PLL or FM-feedback demodulators.

5.3.2 Pre-emphasis and de-emphasis

Because FM noise grows as \(f^2\) while speech/music energy falls with \(f\), a high-frequency boost (pre-emphasis, time constant \(\tau=75\,\mu\)s, corner \(1/2\pi\tau=2.1\) kHz) is applied at the transmitter and a matching low-pass (de-emphasis) after the discriminator. The signal is restored while the output noise is reduced; for \(W=15\) kHz the improvement is \[ I=\frac{(W/f_d)^3}{3\left[(W/f_d)-\arctan(W/f_d)\right]}=21.3\approx13.3\ \text{dB}\quad(f_d=2.1\,\text{kHz}). \]

Power efficiency: SSB and DSB-SC \(>\) AM. Bandwidth efficiency: SSB \(>\) VSB \(>\) AM = DSB \(>\) FM. FM trades bandwidth for noise immunity and also has a constant-envelope signal (non-linear power amplifiers can be used).

Conceptual Trap

(i) FOM for AM is \(\eta\), not \(\mu^2\). (ii) The FM improvement \(\tfrac32\beta^2\) applies only above threshold. (iii) Output noise PSD in FM is parabolic, not flat. (iv) SSB and DSB-SC have identical FOM although SSB uses half the bandwidth. (v) Increasing \(\beta\) increases both \(\text{SNR}_O\) and bandwidth.

GATE Shortcut

FOM table: DSB-SC 1, SSB 1, AM tone \(\mu^2/(2+\mu^2)\), FM tone \(\tfrac32\beta^2\). Convert to dB with \(10\log_{10}\). Output SNR (dB) \(=\text{SNR}_C\) (dB) \(+\) FOM (dB).

5.5 GATE problems

GATE 1-Mark Problems

Q1. The figure of merit of a coherent DSB-SC receiver is (A) 1/3 (B) 1/2 (C) 1 (D) 2.

Q2. The maximum FOM of a tone-modulated AM receiver (envelope detector, \(\mu\le1\)) is (A) 1 (B) 1/2 (C) 1/3 (D) 3/2.

Q3. The noise PSD at an FM discriminator output varies with \(f\) as (A) constant (B) \(f\) (C) \(f^2\) (D) \(1/f\).

Q4. Pre-emphasis in FM is applied to (A) reduce bandwidth (B) improve high-frequency SNR (C) reduce power (D) remove the carrier.

Answers: Q1 (C). Q2 (C). Q3 (C). Q4 (B).

GATE 2-Mark Problems

Q5. Tone FM with \(\beta=5\) has FOM (dB) of (A) 15.7 (B) 7.8 (C) 37.5 (D) 20.5.

Q6. \(\text{SNR}_C=40\) dB. The output SNR of AM with \(\mu=1\) (tone), envelope detector, is (A) 35.2 dB (B) 40 dB (C) 44.8 dB (D) 55.7 dB.

Q7. For tone FM, raising \(\beta\) from 5 to 10 changes the output SNR (above threshold) by (A) \(+3\) dB (B) \(+6\) dB (C) \(+12\) dB (D) none.

Answers: Q5 (A). Q6 (A). Q7 (B).

Numerical Answer Type (NAT)

Q8. Gain (dB, one decimal) of tone FM with \(\beta=5\) over tone AM with \(\mu=1\) at the same \(\text{SNR}_C\): .

Q9. Bandwidth (kHz) for \(\beta=10\), \(W=15\) kHz: .

Answers: Q8: 20.5. Q9: \(2\times11\times15=330\).

Multiple Select (MSQ)

Q10. Select the true statements. (A) Coherent detection has no threshold. (B) FM output noise is flat. (C) FM trades bandwidth for SNR. (D) AM FOM \(\le1/3\) for a tone.

Answers: Q10: (A), (C), (D).

Chapter Test

T1. FOM of SSB.   T2. Effect of de-emphasis on the message.   T3. Threshold carrier-to-noise ratio of an FM discriminator.

Answers: T1: 1. T2: restores the original spectrum. T3: about 10 dB.

One-Page Revision
  • \(\text{SNR}_{base}=P/N_0W\); FOM\(=\text{SNR}_O/\text{SNR}_C\).
  • DSB-SC, SSB: 1. AM: \(\eta\) (\(\le\tfrac13\)). FM tone: \(\tfrac32\beta^2\); threshold \(\approx10\) dB.
  • FM noise PSD \(\propto f^2\); pre-/de-emphasis (\(75\,\mu\)s) gives \(\approx13\) dB.
