GATE Focus

Nyquist rate for composite signals, quantization step and noise power, SQNR (\(6.02n+1.76\) dB), PCM bit rate and bandwidth, companding, slope overload in delta modulation, DPCM prediction gain, and TDM frame rates (T1/E1).

6.1 Sampling

A bandlimited signal \(x(t)\) with highest frequency \(W\) is completely determined by samples taken at \(f_s\ge2W\) (Nyquist). Sampling at rate \(f_s\) replicates the spectrum at multiples of \(f_s\): \(X_s(f)=f_s\sum_kX(f-kf_s)\). If \(f_s<2W\) the replicas overlap: aliasing. Remedies: an anti-aliasing low-pass filter before the sampler, and \(f_s\) somewhat above \(2W\) so that the reconstruction filter is realisable (guard band \(f_s-2W\)).

Nyquist rate

Minimum sampling rate \(=2f_{max}\) (Nyquist rate). For a product or sum of signals use the highest frequency present: \(x_1(t)x_2(t)\) has bandwidth \(W_1+W_2\); \(x^2(t)\) has bandwidth \(2W\); \(x_1(t)+x_2(t)\) has \(\max(W_1,W_2)\).
\(\text{sinc}(at)\) has bandwidth \(a/2\) Hz; \(\text{sinc}^2(at)\) has bandwidth \(a\) Hz.
Bandpass signals: sample at \(f_s\ge2B\) with suitable integer positioning (\(f_s=2f_H/k\), \(k\le\lfloor f_H/B\rfloor\)).

Solved Example

\(x(t)=\cos2000\pi t+\sin4000\pi t\): \(f_{max}=2\) kHz, Nyquist rate \(4\) kHz. \(x(t)=\text{sinc}^2(100t)\): bandwidth \(100\) Hz, Nyquist rate \(200\) Hz. A \(1.5\) kHz tone sampled at \(2\) kHz aliases to \(|1.5-2|=0.5\) kHz.

Reconstruction: ideal low-pass filter with cutoff between \(W\) and \(f_s-W\); practical (zero-order hold) adds \(\text{sinc}(f/f_s)\) droop. Flat-top (sample-and-hold) sampling multiplies the spectrum by \(\text{sinc}(f\tau)\) (aperture effect), corrected by an equaliser; natural sampling multiplies by a pulse train (spectrum envelope \(\propto\text{sinc}\) of the pulse). Pulse-amplitude modulation (PAM) is sampling; PWM and PPM encode amplitude in width or position.

6.2 Quantization

A uniform quantizer maps the sample to the nearest of \(L=2^n\) levels spanning \([-m_p,m_p]\):

Quantization noise

Step size \(\Delta=\dfrac{2m_p}{L}=\dfrac{2m_p}{2^n}\). Error \(\varepsilon\in[-\Delta/2,\Delta/2]\) uniform, so noise power \(\sigma_q^2=\dfrac{\Delta^2}{12}=\dfrac{m_p^2}{3L^2}\).
\(\text{SQNR}=\dfrac{P}{\sigma_q^2}=\dfrac{3L^2P}{m_p^2}\). Full-scale sine (\(P=m_p^2/2\)): \(\text{SQNR}=1.5L^2=6.02n+1.76\) dB. Full-scale uniform: \(L^2\) (\(6.02n\) dB).
Each extra bit gives \(+6\) dB.

Solved Example

8-bit, \(m_p=1\) V: \(\Delta=7.81\) mV, \(\sigma_q^2=5.09\times10^{-6}\,\text{V}^2\), SQNR (sine) \(=49.9\) dB. For SQNR \(\ge50\) dB with a sine: \(n\ge(50-1.76)/6.02=8.01\Rightarrow n=9\). A 10-bit converter over 5 V has \(\Delta=4.88\) mV.

Non-uniform quantization (companding). Weak signals suffer low SQNR with a uniform quantizer. A compressor before and expander after give nearly constant SQNR over a wide dynamic range: \(\mu\)-law (\(\mu=255\), North America/Japan): \(y=\text{sgn}(x)\dfrac{\ln(1+\mu|x|)}{\ln(1+\mu)}\); A-law (\(A=87.6\), Europe).

6.3 Pulse-code modulation

Sample \(\to\) quantize \(\to\) encode (\(n\) bits per sample). The bit rate and bandwidth:

PCM

\(R_b=nf_s\) (bits/s). Minimum channel bandwidth (Nyquist, binary): \(B\ge\dfrac{R_b}2=\dfrac{nf_s}2\).
Voice: \(f_s=8\) kHz, \(n=8\): \(R_b=64\) kbps, \(B\ge32\) kHz.
SQNR improves \(6\) dB per bit, bandwidth increases linearly with \(n\): exponential SNR gain for linear bandwidth cost.

Advantages: regeneration (noise does not accumulate), digital storage, multiplexing. Disadvantages: larger bandwidth, quantization noise, synchronisation. Channel errors: a bit error in the MSB causes a large amplitude error (\(2^{n-1}\Delta\)); total output noise \(=\sigma_q^2+\) error noise.

6.4 Differential PCM and delta modulation

DPCM: quantize the prediction error \(e[k]=m[k]-\hat m[k]\) instead of \(m[k]\); correlated samples give small error variance. Prediction gain \(G_p=\sigma_m^2/\sigma_e^2\); the saving is \(\tfrac12\log_2G_p\) bits per sample at equal SQNR (or \(10\log G_p\) dB at equal bits). \(G_p=12\) dB saves \(12/6.02\approx2\) bits.

Delta modulation (DM): DPCM with a one-bit quantizer and a very high sampling rate; the staircase approximation rises or falls by one step \(\Delta\) each sample according to the sign of the error.

Delta modulation

Maximum trackable slope \(=\Delta f_s\). Slope-overload avoided if \(\Delta f_s\ge\max\left|\dfrac{dm}{dt}\right|\); for \(m=A_m\cos2\pi f_mt\): \(\Delta\ge\dfrac{2\pi f_mA_m}{f_s}\).
Granular noise for small signals grows with \(\Delta\); hence a trade-off. Adaptive DM varies \(\Delta\) with the slope.
Output noise (granular) \(\approx\Delta^2/3\) within the filter bandwidth ratio \(W/f_s\): \(\sigma_q^2=\dfrac{\Delta^2}3\dfrac W{f_s}\).

Solved Example

\(A_m=1\) V, \(f_m=1\) kHz, \(f_s=32\) kHz: \(\Delta\ge2\pi\times10^3/32\times10^3=0.196\) V to avoid slope overload. DM bit rate \(=f_s=32\) kbps (one bit/sample).

6.5 Multiplexing

  • FDM: signals shifted to non-overlapping bands by subcarriers (SSB), guard bands between channels. Telephone: \(4\) kHz spacing; 12-channel group \(60\)–\(108\) kHz.
  • TDM: samples of several channels interleaved in time slots; requires synchronisation (frame bits) and guard times; commutator and decommutator must be in step.
SystemFrameBit rate
T1 (North America)24 channels \(\times\) 8 bits \(+\) 1 framing bit \(=193\) bits per \(125\,\mu\)s\(1.544\) Mbps
E1 (Europe)32 slots \(\times\) 8 bits (30 voice, 1 frame, 1 signalling)\(2.048\) Mbps

For \(N\) channels of bandwidth \(W\) each, TDM sample rate \(=2NW\) and bit rate \(=2NWn\). Multiple access (FDMA, TDMA, CDMA) shares a channel between users; the same ideas apply.

Conceptual Trap

(i) Nyquist rate is \(2f_{max}\), not \(f_{max}\); for products add bandwidths. (ii) Noise power \(\Delta^2/12\) assumes uniform error, no overload. (iii) \(6.02n+1.76\) holds for a full-scale sinusoid only. (iv) PCM bandwidth is proportional to \(n\); SQNR exponential in \(n\). (v) Slope overload in DM relates \(\Delta f_s\) to the slope, not the amplitude.

GATE Shortcut

Voice PCM: \(64\) kbps, \(32\) kHz. \(\text{SQNR}\approx6n+1.8\) dB. T1 \(=24\times64+8=1544\) kbps. DM: \(\Delta=2\pi f_mA_m/f_s\).

6.6 GATE problems

GATE 1-Mark Problems

Q1. The Nyquist rate of \(x(t)=\cos2000\pi t+\sin4000\pi t\) is (A) 2 kHz (B) 4 kHz (C) 6 kHz (D) 8 kHz.

Q2. The Nyquist rate of \(\text{sinc}^2(100t)\) is (A) 100 Hz (B) 200 Hz (C) 400 Hz (D) 50 Hz.

Q3. Each additional bit in PCM improves the SQNR by (A) 3 dB (B) 6 dB (C) 12 dB (D) 1.76 dB.

Q4. The T1 line rate is (A) 1.544 Mbps (B) 2.048 Mbps (C) 64 kbps (D) 8.448 Mbps.

Answers: Q1 (B). Q2 (B). Q3 (B). Q4 (A).

GATE 2-Mark Problems

Q5. A voice signal (4 kHz) is PCM coded with 8 bits/sample at the Nyquist rate. The bit rate and minimum bandwidth are (A) 64 kbps, 32 kHz (B) 32 kbps, 16 kHz (C) 64 kbps, 64 kHz (D) 128 kbps, 64 kHz.

Q6. The minimum number of bits per sample for SQNR \(\ge50\) dB with a full-scale sinusoid is (A) 7 (B) 8 (C) 9 (D) 10.

Q7. A delta modulator: \(m(t)=\cos2\pi\times1000t\) V, \(f_s=32\) kHz. The minimum step size to avoid slope overload is (A) 0.1 V (B) 0.196 V (C) 0.5 V (D) 1 V.

Answers: Q5 (A). Q6 (C): \(8\) bits give \(49.9\) dB. Q7 (B).

Numerical Answer Type (NAT)

Q8. Quantization noise power (in \(10^{-6}\,\text{V}^2\), two decimals) for an 8-bit quantizer with \(m_p=1\) V: .

Q9. A DPCM system achieves prediction gain 12 dB. Bits saved per sample (nearest integer) at equal SQNR: .

Q10. Bit rate (kbps) of a 24-channel T1 frame of 193 bits at 8000 frames/s: .

Answers: Q8: \(5.09\). Q9: 2. Q10: 1544.

Multiple Select (MSQ)

Q11. Select the true statements. (A) Aliasing occurs when \(f_s<2W\). (B) Companding improves the SQNR of weak signals. (C) DM uses one bit per sample. (D) PCM bandwidth is independent of \(n\).

Answers: Q11: (A), (B), (C).

Chapter Test

T1. Alias of a 7 kHz tone sampled at 10 kHz.   T2. \(\Delta\) for 6-bit, \(\pm2\) V.   T3. Why use a high \(f_s\) in DM?

Answers: T1: 3 kHz. T2: \(4/64=62.5\) mV. T3: small step and fine tracking while avoiding slope overload.
