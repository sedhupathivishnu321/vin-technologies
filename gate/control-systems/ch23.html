23.1 Multi-loop block diagrams

Set 23.1

Q1. \(G_1=\dfrac1s\) and \(G_2=\dfrac1{s+1}\) are in cascade; a negative feedback \(H_1=2\) surrounds \(G_2\) only, and a unity negative feedback surrounds the whole. \(C/R=\) (A) \(\dfrac1{s^2+3s+1}\) (B) \(\dfrac1{s^2+3s+3}\) (C) \(\dfrac{1}{s^2+s+1}\) (D) \(\dfrac{1}{s^2+2s+1}\).

Q2. In the nested-loop diagram of Chapter 5 (\(C/R=\dfrac{G_1G_2G_3}{1+G_2H_1+G_1G_2G_3H_2}\)), \(G_1=2,G_2=3,G_3=4,H_1=1,H_2=0.5\). \(C/R=\) (A) \(1\) (B) \(1.5\) (C) \(2\) (D) \(24\).

Q3. \(G_1=1\) and \(G_2=2\) in parallel, followed by \(G_3=\dfrac1{s+1}\), all inside a unity negative feedback. \(C/R=\) (A) \(\dfrac{3}{s+4}\) (B) \(\dfrac3{s+3}\) (C) \(\dfrac{1}{s+4}\) (D) \(\dfrac{3}{s+1}\).

Answers: Q1 (A): inner loop \(\dfrac{G_2}{1+2G_2}=\dfrac1{s+3}\); open loop \(\dfrac1{s(s+3)}\); closed \(\dfrac{1}{s^2+3s+1}\). Q2 (B): \(\dfrac{24}{1+3+12}=1.5\). Q3 (A): forward \(\dfrac{3}{s+1}\); \(\dfrac{3}{s+1+3}\).

23.2 Mason’s gain formula

Set 23.2

Q4. Two forward paths \(P_1=10\) (touching both loops) and \(P_2=5\) (touching only \(L_2\)). Loops \(L_1=-0.4\), \(L_2=-0.5\) do not touch each other. \(T=\) (A) \(6.25\) (B) \(8.10\) (C) \(10.0\) (D) \(7.14\).

Q5. Use the graph of Figure in Chapter 6 with \(G_1=\dots=G_6=1\), \(H_1=1\), \(H_2=2\). \(T=\) (A) \(0.75\) (B) \(0.5\) (C) \(1\) (D) \(0.33\).

Q6. One forward path \(P=6\) touches two touching loops \(L_1=-2\), \(L_2=-1\). \(T=\) (A) \(6/4\) (B) \(6/2\) (C) \(6/6\) (D) \(6/1\).

Answers: Q4 (B): \(\Delta=1+0.4+0.5+0.2=2.1\); \(\Delta_2=1-L_1=1.4\); \(\dfrac{10+5(1.4)}{2.1}=8.10\). Q5 (B): \(\Delta=(1+1)(1+2)=6\); \(P_1\Delta_1=1\); \(P_2\Delta_2=1\cdot2=2\); \(3/6\). Q6 (A): \(\Delta=1+2+1=4\), \(T=6/4=1.5\).

23.3 Higher-order and design-based transient response

Set 23.3

Q7. \(T(s)=\dfrac{100}{(s^2+2s+5)(s+20)}\). Using the dominant-pole approximation, the peak overshoot and \(2\%\) settling time are approximately (A) \(21\%\), \(4\) s (B) \(5\%\), \(1\) s (C) \(16\%\), \(2\) s (D) \(0\%\), \(0.2\) s.

Q8. \(G=\dfrac{K}{s(s+6)}\) in a unity-feedback loop gives \(16.3\%\) overshoot for \(K=\) (A) \(9\) (B) \(18\) (C) \(36\) (D) \(72\).

Q9. A standard second-order system has \(25\%\) overshoot with peak time \(2\) s. Then \(\omega_n\) is approximately (A) \(1.0\) (B) \(1.7\) (C) \(2.5\) (D) \(3.1\) rad/s.

Answers: Q7 (A): dominant poles \(-1\pm j2\) (\(\zeta=0.447\)): \(M_p=e^{-\pi\zeta/\sqrt{1-\zeta^2}}=20.8\%\), \(t_s=4/1=4\) s (the exact peak is \(1.207\)). Q8 (C): \(\zeta=0.5\), \(2\zeta\omega_n=6\Rightarrow\omega_n=6\), \(K=\omega_n^2=36\). Q9 (B): \(\zeta=0.404\), \(\omega_d=\pi/2=1.571\), \(\omega_n=1.571/0.915=1.72\).

23.4 Steady-state error

Set 23.4

Q10. \(G=\dfrac{10(s+2)}{s^2(s+5)}\), unity feedback, input \(r=t^2/2\). \(e_{ss}=\) (A) \(0\) (B) \(0.25\) (C) \(4\) (D) \(\infty\).

Q11. \(G=\dfrac{20}{(s+1)(s+4)}\), unity feedback, \(r=3u(t)\). \(e_{ss}=\) (A) \(0.5\) (B) \(0.6\) (C) \(1\) (D) \(0.25\).

Q12. \(G=\dfrac{10}{s(s+2)}\), \(H=0.5\), \(r=t\). The steady-state actuating error \(e=r-b\) is (A) \(0.2\) (B) \(0.4\) (C) \(0.5\) (D) \(1\).

Answers: Q10 (B): stable (\(s^3+5s^2+10s+20\): \(50>20\)); \(K_a=\dfrac{10\cdot2}{5}=4\). Q11 (A): \(K_p=\dfrac{20}{4}=5\), \(\dfrac{3}{6}\). Q12 (B): \(K_v=\dfrac{10\cdot0.5}{2}=2.5\), \(e=1/2.5\).

23.5 Routh–Hurwitz

Set 23.5

Q13. \(s^4+6s^3+11s^2+6s+K\) is stable for (A) \(0<K<6\) (B) \(0<K<10\) (C) \(K>10\) (D) \(0<K<66\).

Q14. \(1+\dfrac{K}{s(s+3)(s+6)}=0\): the maximum stable \(K\) and the frequency at marginal stability are (A) \(162\), \(4.24\) (B) \(54\), \(3\) (C) \(162\), \(3\) (D) \(18\), \(4.24\).

Q15. The number of RHP roots of \(s^5+2s^4+2s^3+4s^2+11s+10\) is (A) 0 (B) 1 (C) 2 (D) 3.

Answers: Q13 (B): \(s^2\) row: \(10,K\); \(s^1\): \(6-0.6K>0\). At \(K=10\): \(10s^2+10=0\), \(s=\pm j\). Q14 (A): \(s^3+9s^2+18s+K\): \(9\cdot18=162\); \(9s^2+162=0\Rightarrow\omega=\sqrt{18}=4.24\). Q15 (C): rows \(1,2,11\); \(2,4,10\); \(0\to\varepsilon,6\); \(s^2\): \(4-12/\varepsilon<0\); \(s^1\to6\); \(s^0=10\): signs \(+,+,+,-,+,+\): two changes.

23.6 Bode and frequency-domain stability

Set 23.6

Q16. \(G=\dfrac{50}{s(s+5)}\), unity feedback: the phase margin is approximately (A) \(25^\circ\) (B) \(39^\circ\) (C) \(52^\circ\) (D) \(65^\circ\).

Q17. \(G=\dfrac{K}{s(s+2)(s+10)}\) has a gain margin of \(6\) dB for \(K=\) (A) \(60\) (B) \(120\) (C) \(240\) (D) \(30\).

Q18. An asymptotic minimum-phase magnitude plot: \(+40\) dB at \(\omega=0.1\) with slope \(-20\) dB/dec, changing to \(-40\) dB/dec at \(\omega=2\). The velocity error constant is (A) \(2\) (B) \(5\) (C) \(10\) (D) \(20\).

Answers: Q16 (B): \(\omega_{gc}=6.25\); PM \(=90^\circ-\tan^{-1}(1.25)=38.7^\circ\). Q17 (B): \(K_{max}=240\), GM \(=240/K=2\). Q18 (C): \(20\log(K_v/0.1)=40\Rightarrow K_v=10\); \(G=\dfrac{10}{s(1+s/2)}\).

23.7 Root-locus design

Set 23.7

Q19. \(G=\dfrac{K}{s(s+6)}\): for closed-loop poles with \(\zeta=0.707\), \(K=\) (A) \(9\) (B) \(18\) (C) \(36\) (D) \(6\).

Q20. \(G=\dfrac{K(s+4)}{s(s+1)(s+2)(s+3)}\): centroid of the asymptotes is (A) \(-0.67\) (B) \(-2\) (C) \(-1.5\) (D) \(-2.5\).

Q21. \(G=\dfrac{K}{s(s+3)}\): the breakaway point and the gain there are (A) \(-1.5\), \(2.25\) (B) \(-1.5\), \(4.5\) (C) \(-3\), \(2.25\) (D) \(-1\), \(2\).

Answers: Q19 (B): poles \(-3\pm j3\); \(K=|s||s+6|=4.24\times4.24=18\). Q20 (A): \(n-m=3\), \((-6+4)/3=-0.67\) (angles \(60^\circ,180^\circ,300^\circ\)). Q21 (A): \(K=-s(s+3)\), \(dK/ds=0\Rightarrow s=-1.5\), \(K=2.25\).

23.8 Nyquist

Set 23.8

Q22. \(G=\dfrac{3}{s(s+1)(s+2)}\) (unity feedback): the gain margin is (A) \(2\) (B) \(3\) (C) \(6\) (D) \(1\).

Q23. \(P=2\), and the Nyquist plot encircles \(-1\) twice anticlockwise. RHP poles of the closed loop: (A) 0 (B) 2 (C) 4 (D) \(-2\).

Q24. \(G=\dfrac{K}{s-2}\) (unity negative feedback) is stable for (A) \(K<2\) (B) \(K>2\) (C) \(K>0\) (D) all \(K\).

Answers: Q22 (A): \(K_{max}=6\), so GM \(=6/3=2\) (stable, no RHP poles). Q23 (A): \(N=-2\), \(Z=P+N=0\). Q24 (B): \(s-2+K\); the Nyquist circle crosses the real axis at \(-K/2\), enclosing \(-1\) (anticlockwise, \(P=1\)) iff \(K>2\).

23.9 Compensator design

Set 23.9

Q25. A lead compensator must supply \(\phi_m=45^\circ\). Then \(a=p/z=\) (A) \(2.4\) (B) \(4.0\) (C) \(5.8\) (D) \(9\).

Q26. A lag compensator \(\dfrac{s+0.1}{s+0.01}\) multiplies the DC gain by \(10\); a type-1 loop with \(K_v=2\) then has \(K_v=\) (A) \(0.2\) (B) \(2\) (C) \(20\) (D) \(200\).

Q27. For \(G_c=\dfrac{s+3}{s+12}\) the frequency of maximum phase and the phase are (A) \(6\) rad/s, \(36.9^\circ\) (B) \(7.5\) rad/s, \(36.9^\circ\) (C) \(6\) rad/s, \(53^\circ\) (D) \(3\) rad/s, \(30^\circ\).

Answers: Q25 (C): \(\dfrac{1+0.7071}{1-0.7071}=5.83\). Q26 (C): DC gain \(0.1/0.01=10\), so \(K_v=20\). Q27 (A): \(\omega_m=\sqrt{36}=6\); \(a=4\), \(\phi_m=\sin^{-1}0.6\).

23.10 State-transition matrix and state-space

Set 23.10

Q28. \(A=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\-6&-5\end{bmatrix}\): \(\Phi_{11}(t)=\) (A) \(3e^{-2t}-2e^{-3t}\) (B) \(2e^{-2t}-e^{-3t}\) (C) \(e^{-2t}+e^{-3t}\) (D) \(3e^{-2t}+2e^{-3t}\).

Q29. \(A=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\-2&-3\end{bmatrix}\), \(x(0)=[0\ 1]^T\): \(x_2(1)=\) (A) \(-0.097\) (B) \(0.233\) (C) \(0.368\) (D) \(-0.368\).

Q30. \(\Phi(t)=e^{-t}\begin{bmatrix}\cos2t&\sin2t\\-\sin2t&\cos2t\end{bmatrix}\): the matrix \(A\) and its eigenvalues are (A) \(\begin{bmatrix}-1&2\\-2&-1\end{bmatrix}\), \(-1\pm j2\) (B) \(\begin{bmatrix}-1&-2\\2&-1\end{bmatrix}\), \(-1\pm j2\) (C) \(\begin{bmatrix}0&2\\-2&0\end{bmatrix}\), \(\pm j2\) (D) \(\begin{bmatrix}-1&0\\0&-1\end{bmatrix}\), \(-1,-1\).

Answers: Q28 (A): \(\dfrac{s+5}{(s+2)(s+3)}=\dfrac{3}{s+2}-\dfrac2{s+3}\). Q29 (A): second column of \(\Phi\): \(x_2=-e^{-t}+2e^{-2t}\); at \(1\): \(-0.368+0.271=-0.097\). Q30 (A): \(A=\dot\Phi(0)=\begin{bmatrix}-1&2\\-2&-1\end{bmatrix}\).
