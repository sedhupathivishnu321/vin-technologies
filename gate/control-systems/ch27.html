This chapter is a topic-wise map of what GATE ECE repeatedly asks in Control Systems, with worked problems of each type. The problems are original: they reproduce the pattern (structure, level, numbers of the kind used) of past questions, not the questions themselves, and no year or question number is attached. To practise the actual papers, download them from the GATE organising institute’s website, tag each question with the topics below, and use the tracker in Section 27.16. For the topic-wise or year-wise answer keys use the official keys.

27.1 The paper format you must prepare for

TypeMarkingNote
MCQ (1 or 2 marks)wrong answer: \(-\tfrac13\) (1-mark), \(-\tfrac23\) (2-mark)one correct option
MSQ (1 or 2 marks)no negative marking; all correct options neededpartial answers score zero
NAT (1 or 2 marks)no negative markingtype the number; a tolerance range is accepted

Control Systems is typically about \(8\)–\(10\) marks per paper: a mix of \(3\)–\(4\) one-mark and \(3\)–\(4\) two-mark questions. Recurring calculation topics: second-order specifications, error constants, Routh range of \(K\), Bode margins, root-locus features, Nyquist encirclements, state-space (transfer function, STM, controllability).

27.3 Pattern problems: basic control systems

Pattern 27.3

P1. \(y(t)=x(t)\cos t\) is (A) linear, TI (B) linear, TV (C) nonlinear, TI (D) nonlinear, TV.

P2. Negative feedback changes the sensitivity of \(T\) to \(G\) by the factor (A) \(1+GH\) (B) \(\dfrac1{1+GH}\) (C) \(GH\) (D) \(1\).

Answers: P1 (B): time-varying gain \(\cos t\). P2 (B).

27.4 Pattern problems: transfer function and feedback

Pattern 27.4

P3. \(h(t)=te^{-t}u(t)\). \(G(s)=\) (A) \(\dfrac{1}{s+1}\) (B) \(\dfrac1{(s+1)^2}\) (C) \(\dfrac{s}{(s+1)^2}\) (D) \(\dfrac{1}{s^2+1}\).

P4. \(G=\dfrac{10}{s+2}\) with a negative feedback \(H=k\). For the closed-loop time constant to be half the open-loop value, \(k=\) (A) \(0.1\) (B) \(0.2\) (C) \(0.5\) (D) \(1\).

Answers: P3 (B). P4 (B): open-loop \(\tau=0.5\); \(T=\dfrac{10}{s+2+10k}\) with \(\tau=0.25\Rightarrow2+10k=4\).

27.5 Pattern problems: block diagrams and signal flow graphs

Pattern 27.5

P5. \(G_1=1\), \(G_2=\dfrac{1}{s+1}\) with an inner positive feedback \(H_1=0.5\) around \(G_2\) and an outer unity negative feedback around the cascade. \(C/R=\) (A) \(\dfrac1{s+1.5}\) (B) \(\dfrac{1}{s+0.5}\) (C) \(\dfrac1{s+2.5}\) (D) \(\dfrac{1}{s+1}\).

P6. A path \(P=G_1G_2G_3\) with loops \(L_1=-G_2H_1\), \(L_2=-G_3H_2\) (touching) and \(L_3=-G_1G_2G_3H_3\). \(T=\) (A) \(\dfrac{P}{1+G_2H_1+G_3H_2+G_1G_2G_3H_3}\) (B) with an extra \(+G_2G_3H_1H_2\) in \(\Delta\) (C) \(\dfrac{P}{1-\sum L}\) with positive \(L\) (D) \(P(1+\sum L)\).

Answers: P5 (A): inner \(\dfrac{1/(s+1)}{1-0.5/(s+1)}=\dfrac1{s+0.5}\); outer \(\dfrac{1}{s+1.5}\). P6 (A): every loop touches.

27.6 Pattern problems: time response

Pattern 27.6

P7. A standard second-order system has \(16.3\%\) overshoot and \(2\%\) settling time \(2\) s. Its transfer function is (A) \(\dfrac{16}{s^2+4s+16}\) (B) \(\dfrac{4}{s^2+2s+4}\) (C) \(\dfrac{25}{s^2+5s+25}\) (D) \(\dfrac{9}{s^2+3s+9}\).

P8. Step response peaks at \(1.25\) at \(t=2\) s: the damping ratio is (A) \(0.20\) (B) \(0.40\) (C) \(0.60\) (D) \(0.80\).

Answers: P7 (A): \(\zeta=0.5\), \(\zeta\omega_n=2\Rightarrow\omega_n=4\). P8 (B): \(M_p=25\%\Rightarrow\zeta=0.404\).

27.7 Pattern problems: steady-state error

Pattern 27.7

P9. Type-1 unity feedback with \(K_v=5\) and input \(r=2+3t\): \(e_{ss}=\) (A) \(0\) (B) \(0.6\) (C) \(0.4\) (D) \(\infty\).

P10. \(G=\dfrac{K}{s(s+4)}\), \(r=4t\). Minimum \(K\) for \(e_{ss}\le0.2\): (A) \(20\) (B) \(40\) (C) \(80\) (D) \(160\).

Answers: P9 (B): \(0+3/5\). P10 (C): \(e=4/K_v=16/K\le0.2\Rightarrow K\ge80\).

27.8 Pattern problems: Routh–Hurwitz

Pattern 27.8

P11. \(s^3+3s^2+(K+2)s+4K=0\) is stable for (A) \(K<6\) (B) \(0<K<6\) (C) \(K>6\) (D) all \(K>0\).

P12. \(s^4+s^3+3s^2+2s+2=0\) has (A) 4 LHP roots (B) two roots on the \(j\omega\) axis and two in the LHP (C) 2 RHP roots (D) all real roots.

Answers: P11 (B): \(3(K+2)>4K\Rightarrow K<6\), and \(K>0\). P12 (B): zero row, \(A=s^2+2\), roots \(\pm j\sqrt2\); the rest \(s^2+s+1\) stable.

27.9 Pattern problems: Bode plots and margins

Pattern 27.9

P13. \(G=\dfrac{10}{s(1+s/2)}\) asymptotic magnitude: initial slope, and the magnitude at \(\omega=0.1\) (A) \(-20\) dB/dec, \(40\) dB (B) \(-40\) dB/dec, \(40\) dB (C) \(-20\) dB/dec, \(20\) dB (D) \(-20\) dB/dec, \(0\) dB.

P14. \(G=\dfrac{K}{s(s+2)(s+10)}\): for GM \(=20\) dB, \(K=\) (A) \(2.4\) (B) \(24\) (C) \(240\) (D) \(12\).

Answers: P13 (A): \(20\log(10/0.1)=40\) dB. P14 (B): \(K_{max}=240\); GM \(=10\Rightarrow K=24\).

27.10 Pattern problems: root locus

Pattern 27.10

P15. \(G=\dfrac{K}{s(s+2)}\) (unity feedback): the breakaway point and the gain there are (A) \(-1\), \(1\) (B) \(-2\), \(2\) (C) \(-1\), \(2\) (D) \(-0.5\), \(0.25\).

P16. Poles at \(0,-2\pm j\) (no zeros): the angle of departure from \(-2+j\) is (A) \(-63.4^\circ\) (B) \(63.4^\circ\) (C) \(-26.6^\circ\) (D) \(90^\circ\).

Answers: P15 (A): \(K=-s(s+2)\), \(s=-1\), \(K=1\). P16 (A): vector from \(0\): \(180^\circ-26.6^\circ=153.4^\circ\); from \(-2-j\): \(90^\circ\); \(\phi_d=180-(153.4+90)=-63.4^\circ\).

27.11 Pattern problems: Nyquist

Pattern 27.11

P17. \(G=\dfrac{K}{(s+1)^3}\): the closed loop is stable for \(K<\) (A) \(4\) (B) \(8\) (C) \(16\) (D) \(2\).

P18. \(P=1\), and the Nyquist plot encircles \(-1\) once anticlockwise: \(Z=\) (A) 0 (B) 1 (C) 2 (D) \(-1\).

Answers: P17 (B). P18 (A): \(Z=1+(-1)\).

27.12 Pattern problems: controllers and compensators

Pattern 27.12

P19. PI \(K_p(1+1/s)\), \(K_p=4\), on the plant \(\dfrac1{s+1}\) in a unity loop: ramp error (A) \(0\) (B) \(0.25\) (C) \(1\) (D) \(4\).

P20. Lead \(\dfrac{s+2}{s+20}\): maximum phase is (A) \(36.9^\circ\) (B) \(45^\circ\) (C) \(54.9^\circ\) (D) \(60^\circ\).

Answers: P19 (B): loop \(\dfrac{4}{s}\), \(K_v=4\). P20 (C): \(a=10\), \(\phi_m=\sin^{-1}\frac{9}{11}=54.9^\circ\) (at \(\omega_m=\sqrt{40}=6.32\) rad/s).

27.13 Pattern problems: state-space

Pattern 27.13

P21. \(A=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\-9&-6\end{bmatrix}\): \(\Phi_{11}(t)=\) (A) \((1+3t)e^{-3t}\) (B) \(e^{-3t}\) (C) \((1-3t)e^{-3t}\) (D) \(3te^{-3t}\).

P22. \(A=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\0&2\end{bmatrix}\), \(B=\begin{bmatrix}1\\k\end{bmatrix}\) is controllable for (A) \(k=0\) (B) \(k\ne0\) (C) \(k=1\) only (D) all \(k\).

Answers: P21 (A): \(\dfrac{s+6}{(s+3)^2}=\dfrac1{s+3}+\dfrac3{(s+3)^2}\). P22 (B): \(\det Q_c=k\).

27.14 Multiple-select and numerical-answer patterns

MSQ patterns

M1. Which are BIBO stable? (A) \(\dfrac1{s+3}\) (B) \(\dfrac{s}{s^2+9}\) (C) \(\dfrac1{s^2+2s+5}\) (D) \(\dfrac{1}{s(s+1)}\).

M2. A lag compensator (A) reduces bandwidth (B) increases \(K_v\) relative to a gain-limited design (C) adds positive phase (D) attenuates noise.

Answers: M1: (A), (C). M2: (A), (B), (D).

NAT patterns

N1. \(G=\dfrac{10}{s(s+1)(s+5)}\), unity feedback: GM in dB (two decimals) .

N2. \(\zeta=0.6\), \(\omega_n=5\): \(t_p=\) s (three decimals).

Answers: N1: \(K_{max}=30\), GM \(=3\to9.54\). N2: \(\omega_d=4\), \(t_p=\pi/4=0.785\).

27.15 How to use the official papers

  1. Solve each paper under exam conditions (Control Systems: about \(10\)–\(12\) minutes for \(8\)–\(10\) marks).
  2. For every question note the topic (Section 27.2), question type (MCQ/MSQ/NAT), and the chapter that solves it.
  3. Redo the wrong ones after one week without looking at the solution.
  4. Older papers lean on formula recall and concepts; recent papers mix in more NAT/MSQ and longer multi-step calculations. Prepare both.
