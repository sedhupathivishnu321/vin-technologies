GATE Focus

The four equations in differential and integral form, displacement current, the continuity equation, constitutive relations, time-harmonic forms, conductor-versus-dielectric behaviour via the loss tangent, and the derivation of the wave equation.

4.1 Maxwell’s equations

General (time-varying) form
LawDifferentialIntegral
Faraday\(\grad\times\vE=-\dfrac{\partial\vB}{\partial t}\)\(\oint\vE\cdot d\mathbf l=-\dfrac d{dt}\iint\vB\cdot d\mathbf S\)
Ampère–Maxwell\(\grad\times\vH=\vJ+\dfrac{\partial\vD}{\partial t}\)\(\oint\vH\cdot d\mathbf l=I+\dfrac d{dt}\iint\vD\cdot d\mathbf S\)
Gauss (electric)\(\grad\cdot\vD=\rho_v\)\(\oiint\vD\cdot d\mathbf S=Q_{enc}\)
Gauss (magnetic)\(\grad\cdot\vB=0\)\(\oiint\vB\cdot d\mathbf S=0\)

Maxwell’s addition is the displacement current density \(\vJ_d=\partial\vD/\partial t\), which lets a capacitor “conduct” and makes waves possible. Without it \(\grad\cdot(\grad\times\vH)=0\) would contradict the continuity equation.

4.2 Continuity equation

Charge conservation

\(\grad\cdot\vJ=-\dfrac{\partial\rho_v}{\partial t}\ \Leftrightarrow\ \oiint\vJ\cdot d\mathbf S=-\dfrac{dQ}{dt}\). It follows from taking the divergence of Ampère–Maxwell and using Gauss’s law.
For \(\vJ=\sigma\vE\) in a conductor: \(\rho_v(t)=\rho_0e^{-t/\tau}\) with relaxation time \(\tau=\eps/\sigma\).

4.3 Constitutive relations

\(\vD=\eps\vE\), \(\vB=\mu\vH\), \(\vJ=\sigma\vE\) (Ohm’s law), \(\eps=\eps_r\epsz\), \(\mu=\mu_r\muz\). A medium is linear if these hold with constants, homogeneous if the constants do not vary with position, isotropic if \(\eps\) is a scalar. In a perfect conductor \(\vE=0\) inside; in a perfect dielectric \(\sigma=0\).

4.4 Time-harmonic fields

With \(e^{j\omega t}\) and phasors:

Phasor form

\(\grad\times\vE=-j\omega\mu\vH\), \(\ \grad\times\vH=(\sigma+j\omega\eps)\vE\), \(\ \grad\cdot\vD=\rho_v\), \(\ \grad\cdot\vB=0\).
Ratio of conduction to displacement current: \(\dfrac{|\vJ_c|}{|\vJ_d|}=\dfrac\sigma{\omega\eps}=\tan\delta_c\) (loss tangent).
Good conductor: \(\sigma/\omega\eps\gg1\); good dielectric: \(\sigma/\omega\eps\ll1\); quasi-conductor in between.

Solved Example

Copper (\(\sigma=5.8\times10^7\) S/m) at \(1\,\)MHz: \(\sigma/\omega\epsz=\frac{5.8\times10^7}{2\pi\times10^6\times8.854\times10^{-12}}\approx10^{12}\), so copper is an excellent conductor and displacement current is negligible. Sea water (\(\sigma=4\), \(\eps_r=81\)) at \(1\,\)MHz: \(\sigma/\omega\eps=\frac{4}{2\pi\times10^6\times81\times8.854\times10^{-12}}\approx888\), a good conductor at that frequency. Relaxation time of copper: \(\frac{8.854\times10^{-12}}{5.8\times10^7}=1.5\times10^{-19}\) s.

4.5 Wave equation

Source-free lossless medium

Take the curl of \(\grad\times\vE=-\mu\,\partial\vH/\partial t\) and use \(\grad\times\vH=\eps\,\partial\vE/\partial t\): \(\grad\times\grad\times\vE=-\mu\eps\,\partial^2\vE/\partial t^2\). With \(\grad\cdot\vE=0\) and the identity \(\grad\times\grad\times\vE=\grad(\grad\cdot\vE)-\grad^2\vE\), \[ \grad^2\vE=\mu\eps\frac{\partial^2\vE}{\partial t^2},\qquad v=\frac1{\sqrt{\mu\eps}}. \] The same holds for \(\vH\). In free space \(v=c=3\times10^8\) m/s.

Helmholtz equation (phasors)

\(\grad^2\vE=\gamma^2\vE\), \(\ \gamma=\alpha+j\beta=\sqrt{j\omega\mu(\sigma+j\omega\eps)}\). Lossless: \(\gamma=j\beta\), \(\beta=\omega\sqrt{\mu\eps}\) and \(\grad^2\vE+\beta^2\vE=0\).

4.6 Boundary conditions (time-varying)

At any interface: \(\ar_n\times(\vE_1-\vE_2)=0\), \(\ \ar_n\times(\vH_1-\vH_2)=\mathbf K\), \(\ \ar_n\cdot(\vD_1-\vD_2)=\rho_s\), \(\ \ar_n\cdot(\vB_1-\vB_2)=0\). At a perfect conductor: \(E_t=0\), \(B_n=0\), \(\ar_n\times\vH=\mathbf K\), \(D_n=\rho_s\).

Conceptual Trap

(i) \(\grad\times\vH=\vJ\) alone is the static law; the displacement-current term is needed for time-varying fields. (ii) The loss tangent compares conduction with displacement current, so the same material is a conductor at low \(f\) and a dielectric at high \(f\). (iii) Relaxation time is \(\eps/\sigma\), not \(\sigma/\eps\). (iv) \(\grad\cdot\vB=0\) holds always.

GATE Shortcut

Identify the law by what it contains: a curl of \(\vE\) with \(\partial\vB/\partial t\) is Faraday; a curl of \(\vH\) is Ampère–Maxwell; a divergence of \(\vB\) is always zero. For a capacitor \(i_d=C\,dv/dt\) equals the conduction current in the wire. For a plane wave in a lossless medium \(|\vE|/|\vH|=\sqrt{\mu/\eps}\).

4.7 GATE problems

GATE 1-Mark Problems

Q1. The equation expressing the absence of magnetic monopoles is (A) \(\grad\cdot\vD=\rho_v\) (B) \(\grad\cdot\vB=0\) (C) \(\grad\times\vE=-\partial\vB/\partial t\) (D) \(\grad\times\vH=\vJ\).

Q2. The displacement current density is (A) \(\sigma\vE\) (B) \(\partial\vD/\partial t\) (C) \(\partial\vB/\partial t\) (D) \(\rho_v\mathbf u\).

Q3. The continuity equation is (A) \(\grad\cdot\vJ=-\partial\rho_v/\partial t\) (B) \(\grad\cdot\vJ=\rho_v\) (C) \(\grad\times\vJ=0\) (D) \(\vJ=\sigma\vE\).

Q4. The equation \(\grad\times\vE=-\partial\vB/\partial t\) is (A) Gauss’s law (B) Ampère’s law (C) Faraday’s law (D) the continuity equation.

Answers: Q1 (B). Q2 (B). Q3 (A). Q4 (C).

GATE 2-Mark Problems

Q5. For copper at \(1\,\)MHz the ratio of conduction to displacement current density is of the order (A) \(10^{-12}\) (B) \(1\) (C) \(10^{6}\) (D) \(10^{12}\).

Q6. Sea water (\(\sigma=4\) S/m, \(\eps_r=81\)) at \(1\,\)MHz has loss tangent about (A) 0.9 (B) 9 (C) 90 (D) 890.

Q7. In free space \(\vE=E_0\cos(\omega t-\beta z)\ar_x\) with \(E_0=377\) V/m. The amplitude of \(\vH\) is (A) 1 A/m (B) 377 A/m (C) 0.5 A/m (D) 142 A/m.

Answers: Q5 (D). Q6 (D). Q7 (A): \(E_0/\eta_0\).

Numerical Answer Type (NAT)

Q8. Relaxation time of copper in units of \(10^{-19}\) s (\(\sigma=5.8\times10^7\) S/m, \(\eps=\epsz\)): .

Q9. In air \(\vE=100\sin(10^9t)\) V/m. Peak displacement current density (A/m\({}^2\)): .

Q10. A \(1\,\mu\)F capacitor has \(v=10\sin(1000t)\) V. Peak displacement current between its plates (mA): .

Answers: Q8: \(1.53\). Q9: \(\epsz\cdot100\cdot10^9=0.885\). Q10: \(C\,dv/dt=10^{-6}\cdot10\cdot1000=10\) mA.

Multiple Select (MSQ)

Q11. Which are part of Maxwell’s equations in a source-free region? (A) \(\grad\cdot\vD=0\) (B) \(\grad\cdot\vB=0\) (C) \(\grad\times\vE=-\partial\vB/\partial t\) (D) \(\grad\times\vH=\sigma\vE\) only (no displacement term).

Answers: Q11: (A), (B), (C).

Chapter Test

T1. Write \(\gamma\) for a conducting medium.   T2. Which Maxwell term did Maxwell add?   T3. Loss tangent?

Answers: T1: \(\sqrt{j\omega\mu(\sigma+j\omega\eps)}\). T2: \(\partial\vD/\partial t\). T3: \(\sigma/\omega\eps\).

One-Page Revision
  • Faraday, Ampère–Maxwell, Gauss (electric), Gauss (magnetic) in both forms.
  • \(\vJ_d=\partial\vD/\partial t\); \(\grad\cdot\vJ=-\partial\rho_v/\partial t\); \(\tau=\eps/\sigma\).
  • Wave equation \(\grad^2\vE=\mu\eps\,\partial^2\vE/\partial t^2\), \(v=1/\sqrt{\mu\eps}\).
  • \(\gamma=\sqrt{j\omega\mu(\sigma+j\omega\eps)}\); \(\tan\delta_c=\sigma/\omega\eps\).
  • Boundary: \(E_t\), \(B_n\) continuous; \(D_n\) jumps by \(\rho_s\); \(H_t\) by \(K\).
