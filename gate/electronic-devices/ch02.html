2.1 Drift current and mobility

Under an electric field \(\mathcal E\) carriers acquire an average drift velocity \(v_d=\mu\mathcal E\), where the mobility \(\mu\) (cm\({}^2\)/V s) measures how freely carriers move between scattering events: \(\mu=q\tau_c/m^*\) (\(\tau_c\) mean time between collisions).

Drift current

\(J_n=q\,n\,\mu_n\mathcal E,\quad J_p=q\,p\,\mu_p\mathcal E\).
Conductivity \(\sigma=q(n\mu_n+p\mu_p)\); resistivity \(\rho=1/\sigma\).
Bar of length \(L\) and area \(A\): \(R=\rho L/A\).
Extrinsic n-type: \(\sigma\approx qN_D\mu_n\); intrinsic: \(\sigma_i=qn_i(\mu_n+\mu_p)\).
Minimum conductivity at \(n=n_i\sqrt{\mu_p/\mu_n}\) (not at \(n=p\)).

Silicon numbers

\(\mu_n=1350\), \(\mu_p=480\ \text{cm}^2/\text{V\,s}\). For \(N_D=10^{16}\): \(\sigma=1.6\times10^{-19}\times10^{16}\times1350=2.16\ \text{S/cm}\), \(\rho=0.462\,\Omega\,\)cm. Intrinsic: \(\sigma_i=1.6\times10^{-19}\times1.5\times10^{10}\times1830=4.4\times10^{-6}\) S/cm, \(\rho_i=2.3\times10^5\,\Omega\,\)cm.
A bar \(L=1\) mm, cross-section \(10\,\mu\text{m}\times10\,\mu\text{m}\) of this n-type silicon: \(R=0.462\times0.1/10^{-6}=46.2\,\)k\(\Omega\).
Drift velocity at \(\mathcal E=1\,\)kV/cm: \(v_d=1350\times10^3=1.35\times10^6\) cm/s. At very high field \(v_d\) saturates at \(v_{sat}\approx10^7\) cm/s in silicon.

2.1.1 Scattering and the temperature dependence of mobility

  • Lattice (phonon) scattering: \(\mu_L\propto T^{-3/2}\) dominates at high \(T\).
  • Ionised-impurity scattering: \(\mu_I\propto T^{3/2}/N_I\) dominates at low \(T\) and high doping.

Matthiessen’s rule: \(\dfrac1\mu=\dfrac1{\mu_L}+\dfrac1{\mu_I}\). Mobility therefore falls with increasing doping and, in the extrinsic range, falls with increasing temperature.

A metal’s resistivity increases with temperature (more scattering, fixed carriers); an intrinsic or lightly doped semiconductor’s resistivity decreases with temperature over most of the range. Hence semiconductors have a negative temperature coefficient of resistivity at high \(T\) and low doping.

2.2 Diffusion current and the Einstein relation

Carriers diffuse from high to low concentration:

Diffusion

\(J_n=q\,D_n\dfrac{dn}{dx},\qquad J_p=-q\,D_p\dfrac{dp}{dx}\).
Total: \(J_n=q\mu_nn\mathcal E+qD_n\dfrac{dn}{dx}\), \(\ J_p=q\mu_pp\mathcal E-qD_p\dfrac{dp}{dx}\).
Einstein relation: \(\dfrac{D}{\mu}=\dfrac{kT}{q}=V_T=0.0259\,\)V at 300 K.
Diffusion length: \(L=\sqrt{D\tau}\).

In Si: \(D_n=0.0259\times1350=34.9\,\text{cm}^2/\text{s}\), \(D_p=0.0259\times480=12.4\,\text{cm}^2/\text{s}\). For \(\tau=1\,\mu\)s: \(L_n=\sqrt{34.9\times10^{-6}}=59\,\mu\)m.

Diffusion current

\(n(x)\) falls linearly from \(10^{16}\) to \(10^{15}\,\text{cm}^{-3}\) over \(1\,\mu\)m \((10^{-4}\) cm\()\): \(dn/dx=-9\times10^{19}\,\text{cm}^{-4}\). \(J_n=1.6\times10^{-19}\times34.9\times(-9\times10^{19})\approx-503\ \text{A/cm}^2\) (electrons diffuse toward \(+x\), so the conventional current points toward \(-x\)). Magnitude \(503\,\)A/cm\({}^2\).

Built-in field in a non-uniformly doped bar. In equilibrium the net current is zero: \(q\mu_nn\mathcal E+qD_n\,dn/dx=0\Rightarrow\mathcal E=-\dfrac{kT}{q}\dfrac1n\dfrac{dn}{dx}\). Electron concentration falling with \(x\) gives a field pointing in \(+x\); this graded-doping field is used in drift transistors.

2.3 Hall effect

A bar carrying current \(I\) in a perpendicular magnetic field \(B\) develops a transverse voltage \[ V_H=\frac{IB}{q\,n\,t}\quad(\text{n-type, } t=\text{thickness along }B),\qquad R_H=\mp\frac1{qn}\ \text{or}\ \frac1{qp}. \] The sign of \(V_H\) gives the carrier type; \(\mu=|R_H|\sigma\) gives the (Hall) mobility. Example: \(I=1\,\)mA, \(B=0.1\,\)T, \(n=10^{22}\,\text{m}^{-3}\), \(t=0.5\,\)mm: \(V_H=\dfrac{10^{-3}\times0.1}{1.6\times10^{-19}\times10^{22}\times5\times10^{-4}}=0.125\,\)mV.

Conceptual Trap

(i) \(D/\mu=kT/q\) holds for both carriers but not for degenerate material. (ii) Drift current is proportional to carrier concentration, diffusion current to its gradient. (iii) Conductivity of an extrinsic sample decreases with temperature (mobility), not increases. (iv) The sign of \(dn/dx\) and the definition of conventional current: for electrons \(J_n=+qD_n\,dn/dx\). (v) Use cm or m consistently: \(\mu\) in cm\({}^2\)/V s with \(n\) in cm\({}^{-3}\).

GATE Shortcut

\(\sigma=q n\mu\): for \(n=10^{16}\) in Si, \(\sigma\approx2.2\) S/cm; \(\rho(\Omega\,\text{cm})\approx0.46\cdot10^{16}/N_D\) for n-type, \(1.3\cdot10^{16}/N_A\) for p-type (low doping). \(D=\mu\times0.0259\).

2.4 GATE problems

GATE 1-Mark Problems

Q1. The Einstein relation at 300 K gives \(D_n/\mu_n=\) (A) 0.0259 V (B) 0.259 V (C) 1 V (D) 0.7 V.

Q2. In the extrinsic region the conductivity of silicon with increasing temperature (A) increases (B) decreases (C) is constant (D) becomes zero.

Q3. Drift current density is proportional to (A) \(dn/dx\) (B) \(n\mathcal E\) (C) \(\mathcal E^2\) (D) \(1/n\).

Q4. Lattice-scattering limited mobility varies with temperature as (A) \(T^{3/2}\) (B) \(T^{-3/2}\) (C) \(T^{0}\) (D) \(e^{-1/T}\).

Answers: Q1 (A). Q2 (B). Q3 (B). Q4 (B).

GATE 2-Mark Problems

Q5. The resistivity of n-type Si with \(N_D=10^{16}\,\text{cm}^{-3}\), \(\mu_n=1350\) is (A) 0.46 \(\Omega\) cm (B) 2.2 \(\Omega\) cm (C) 4.6 \(\Omega\) cm (D) 0.046 \(\Omega\) cm.

Q6. Electrons: \(D_n=35\,\text{cm}^2/\text{s}\), concentration drops linearly from \(10^{16}\) to \(10^{15}\) over \(1\,\mu\)m. The diffusion current density magnitude is (A) 50 (B) 500 (C) 5000 (D) 50 000 A/cm\({}^2\) (use \(q=1.6\times10^{-19}\)).

Q7. Minority-carrier lifetime \(\tau=1\,\mu\)s and \(D=35\,\text{cm}^2/\text{s}\). The diffusion length is (A) 5.9 \(\mu\)m (B) 59 \(\mu\)m (C) 590 \(\mu\)m (D) 6 mm.

Answers: Q5 (A). Q6 (B): \(1.6\times10^{-19}\times35\times9\times10^{19}=504\). Q7 (B): \(\sqrt{35\times10^{-6}}=5.9\times10^{-3}\) cm.

Numerical Answer Type (NAT)

Q8. Resistance of the bar \(L=1\) mm, \(A=10^{-6}\,\text{cm}^2\) with \(\rho=0.462\,\Omega\) cm (k\(\Omega\), one decimal): .

Q9. Hall voltage (mV) for \(I=1\) mA, \(B=0.1\) T, \(n=10^{22}\,\text{m}^{-3}\), \(t=0.5\) mm: (three decimals).

Q10. Intrinsic Si resistivity in k\(\Omega\) cm for \(n_i=1.5\times10^{10}\), \(\mu_n=1350\), \(\mu_p=480\): .

Answers: Q8: 46.2. Q9: 0.125. Q10: 227.

Multiple Select (MSQ)

Q11. Select true statements. (A) Mobility decreases with doping. (B) Diffusion current needs a concentration gradient. (C) The Hall coefficient of p-type is positive. (D) Drift velocity grows without limit with field.

Answers: Q11: (A), (B), (C).

Chapter Test

T1. \(D_p\) in Si (\(\mu_p=480\)).   T2. \(\sigma\) for \(N_A=10^{17}\) (\(\mu_p=300\)).   T3. Why does \(\rho\) of a metal rise with \(T\)?

Answers: T1: 12.4 cm\({}^2\)/s. T2: \(4.8\) S/cm. T3: more phonon scattering with fixed carrier density.

One-Page Revision
  • \(J=q(n\mu_n+p\mu_p)\mathcal E\); \(\sigma=q(n\mu_n+p\mu_p)\); \(R=\rho L/A\).
  • \(J_n^{diff}=qD_n\,dn/dx\); \(D/\mu=kT/q\); \(L=\sqrt{D\tau}\).
