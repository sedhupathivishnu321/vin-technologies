3.1 Generation and recombination

In equilibrium thermal generation and recombination balance: \(G_{th}=R_{th}\), so \(np=n_i^2\). A disturbance (light, injection, bias) creates excess carriers \(\Delta n=\Delta p\) with \(np>n_i^2\) and the semiconductor relaxes back by recombination.

MechanismProcessWhere important
Band-to-band (radiative)electron falls from \(E_C\) to \(E_V\), photon emitteddirect-gap (GaAs, LEDs)
SRH (trap-assisted)via a defect level near mid-gapsilicon and germanium
Augerenergy given to a third carriervery high doping or injection
Surface recombinationdangling bonds at the surfacesolar cells, small devices

Trap levels near mid-gap are the most effective recombination centres, since both capture steps (electron and hole) are then equally likely.

Low-level injection

Net recombination rate of minority carriers: \(U=\dfrac{\Delta p}{\tau_p}\) (n-type), \(\ \dfrac{\Delta n}{\tau_n}\) (p-type).
Decay after the source is removed: \(\Delta p(t)=\Delta p(0)\,e^{-t/\tau_p}\).
Steady-state uniform generation \(G\) (cm\({}^{-3}\)s\({}^{-1}\)): \(\Delta n=\Delta p=G\,\tau\).
SRH lifetime: \(\tau=\dfrac{1}{\sigma v_{th}N_t}\).
Low-level injection means \(\Delta p\ll\) the majority density.

3.1.1 Quasi-Fermi levels

Out of equilibrium a single \(E_F\) cannot describe both carriers; define separate quasi-Fermi levels \(F_n\) and \(F_p\): \[ n=n_ie^{(F_n-E_i)/kT},\quad p=n_ie^{(E_i-F_p)/kT},\quad np=n_i^2e^{(F_n-F_p)/kT}. \] Under forward bias or illumination \(F_n>F_p\) in the active region; \(F_n-F_p=qV\) across a forward-biased junction.

3.2 Photoconductivity

Light with \(h\nu\ge E_g\) generates pairs at rate \(G=\eta P_{opt}/(h\nu\,\text{Vol})\). The conductivity change is \[ \Delta\sigma=q\,\Delta n\,(\mu_n+\mu_p)=q\,G\tau\,(\mu_n+\mu_p). \]

Photoconductor

\(G=10^{18}\,\text{cm}^{-3}\text{s}^{-1}\), \(\tau=1\,\mu\)s: \(\Delta n=10^{12}\,\text{cm}^{-3}\); \(\Delta\sigma=1.6\times10^{-19}\times10^{12}\times1830=2.93\times10^{-4}\,\)S/cm. After the light is switched off the current decays as \(e^{-t/\tau}\); after \(2\tau\) only \(e^{-2}=13.5\%\) of the excess remains.

3.3 The continuity equation

Conservation of carriers in a small volume gives

Continuity equations

\(\dfrac{\partial n}{\partial t}=\dfrac1q\dfrac{\partial J_n}{\partial x}+G_n-U_n,\qquad \dfrac{\partial p}{\partial t}=-\dfrac1q\dfrac{\partial J_p}{\partial x}+G_p-U_p\).
Minority-carrier diffusion equation (n-type, no field, \(G=0\), steady state): \[ D_p\frac{d^2\Delta p}{dx^2}-\frac{\Delta p}{\tau_p}=0\ \Longrightarrow\ \Delta p(x)=Ae^{-x/L_p}+Be^{x/L_p},\quad L_p=\sqrt{D_p\tau_p}. \] Semi-infinite region, \(\Delta p(0)=\Delta p_0\): \(\Delta p(x)=\Delta p_0\,e^{-x/L_p}\).
Short region of width \(W\ll L_p\) with an ideal sink at \(x=W\): \(\Delta p(x)\approx\Delta p_0(1-x/W)\) (linear), giving the diffusion current \(J_p=qD_p\Delta p_0/W\).

Diffusion profile

\(\Delta p_0=10^{14}\,\text{cm}^{-3}\), \(L_p=50\,\mu\)m. At \(x=100\,\mu\)m: \(\Delta p=10^{14}e^{-2}=1.35\times10^{13}\). With \(\tau_p=0.5\,\mu\)s and \(D_p=12.4\): \(L_p=\sqrt{12.4\times0.5\times10^{-6}}=24.9\,\mu\)m.
The minority diffusion current at \(x\): \(J_p(x)=qD_p\Delta p_0e^{-x/L_p}/L_p\), which decays with the same length: this is the origin of the exponential term in the diode equation.

Haynes–Shockley experiment. A pulse of minority carriers is injected in a bar with a drift field; the peak arrival time gives \(\mu=L/(t_d\mathcal E)\), the pulse spreading gives \(D\), and the decay in pulse height gives \(\tau\).

3.4 Poisson’s equation

Gauss’s law in one dimension, with \(\mathcal E=-d\phi/dx\):

Poisson

\(\dfrac{d\mathcal E}{dx}=\dfrac{\rho}{\varepsilon_s},\qquad \dfrac{d^2\phi}{dx^2}=-\dfrac{\rho}{\varepsilon_s},\qquad \rho=q\,(p-n+N_D^+-N_A^-)\).
In the depletion region (\(n,p\approx0\)): \(\rho=q(N_D-N_A)\), a constant in each uniformly doped side.
Procedure: \(\rho(x)\to\mathcal E(x)=\int\rho/\varepsilon\,dx\to\phi(x)=-\int\mathcal E\,dx\) with boundary conditions at the edges of the depletion layer.

These three equations together (Poisson, continuity for electrons and holes, with the current equations) are the complete set of semiconductor device equations; every device chapter in this book solves a simplified form.

Conceptual Trap

(i) Lifetime applies to minority carriers in low-level injection. (ii) \(L=\sqrt{D\tau}\) is a length, not a velocity. (iii) \(np=n_i^2\) no longer holds under injection. (iv) In the short-diode limit the profile is linear, not exponential. (v) Sign of \(\rho\): donors give positive space charge.

GATE Shortcut

Decay questions: fraction left \(=e^{-t/\tau}\). Steady photogeneration: \(\Delta n=G\tau\). Diffusion profile: multiply by \(e^{-x/L}\). Poisson: integrate charge twice, constants from boundary conditions.

3.5 GATE problems

GATE 1-Mark Problems

Q1. After the light is removed excess minority carriers decay with time constant (A) the dielectric relaxation time (B) the minority-carrier lifetime (C) the transit time (D) the majority-carrier lifetime.

Q2. The most effective recombination centres in silicon are located (A) near \(E_C\) (B) near \(E_V\) (C) near mid-gap (D) in the conduction band.

Q3. Minority-carrier diffusion length is (A) \(D\tau\) (B) \(\sqrt{D/\tau}\) (C) \(\sqrt{D\tau}\) (D) \(\mu\tau\).

Q4. Poisson’s equation in one dimension is (A) \(d\mathcal E/dx=\rho/\varepsilon\) (B) \(d\mathcal E/dx=-\rho/\varepsilon\) (C) \(dJ/dx=0\) (D) \(d\mathcal E/dx=\varepsilon\rho\).

Answers: Q1 (B). Q2 (C). Q3 (C). Q4 (A).

GATE 2-Mark Problems

Q5. \(\Delta p(0)=10^{14}\,\text{cm}^{-3}\), \(L_p=50\,\mu\)m, semi-infinite n-type region. \(\Delta p\) at \(100\,\mu\)m (cm\({}^{-3}\)): (A) \(1.35\times10^{13}\) (B) \(3.7\times10^{13}\) (C) \(5\times10^{13}\) (D) \(10^{12}\).

Q6. Uniform generation \(G=10^{18}\,\text{cm}^{-3}\text{s}^{-1}\) and \(\tau=1\,\mu\)s in Si with \(\mu_n=1350\), \(\mu_p=480\). The photoconductivity change (S/cm) is (A) \(2.9\times10^{-4}\) (B) \(2.9\times10^{-3}\) (C) \(1.6\times10^{-4}\) (D) \(3.0\times10^{-5}\).

Q7. In low-level injection, an excess density of \(10^{14}\) pairs in p-type silicon (\(N_A=10^{16}\)) gives \(F_n-F_p\) equal to (A) 0.58 eV (B) 0.25 eV (C) 0.93 eV (D) 0.12 eV (\(kT=0.0259\), \(n_i=1.5\times10^{10}\)).

Answers: Q5 (A). Q6 (A). Q7 (A): \(np/n_i^2=(10^{14})(1.01\times10^{16})/2.25\times10^{20}=4.49\times10^9\); \(0.0259\ln(4.49\times10^9)=0.576\).

Numerical Answer Type (NAT)

Q8. Diffusion length (µ m, one decimal) for \(D_p=12.4\,\text{cm}^2/\text{s}\) and \(\tau_p=0.5\,\mu\)s: .

Q9. Percentage of excess carriers remaining after \(t=3\tau\) (two decimals): .

Answers: Q8: 24.9. Q9: \(e^{-3}=4.98\%\).

Multiple Select (MSQ)

Q10. Which statements hold in low-level injection? (A) Lifetime is that of minority carriers. (B) \(np=n_i^2\) still holds. (C) Majority concentration is nearly unchanged. (D) Net recombination rate \(\approx\Delta p/\tau_p\) in n-type.

Answers: Q10: (A), (C), (D).

Chapter Test

T1. Write the minority diffusion equation.   T2. Define \(F_n,F_p\).   T3. Source of exponential behaviour of diode current.

Answers: T1: \(D_pd^2\Delta p/dx^2-\Delta p/\tau_p=0\). T2: quasi-Fermi levels giving \(n\) and \(p\) out of equilibrium. T3: minority diffusion profile \(e^{-x/L}\) with boundary \(e^{V/V_T}\).

One-Page Revision
  • Recombination: radiative (direct gap), SRH (traps near mid-gap), Auger; \(U=\Delta p/\tau_p\).
  • \(\Delta p(t)=\Delta p_0e^{-t/\tau}\); \(\Delta n=G\tau\); \(\Delta\sigma=q\Delta n(\mu_n+\mu_p)\).
  • Continuity \(\to\) \(D\,\Delta p''=\Delta p/\tau\); \(L=\sqrt{D\tau}\).
  • Poisson \(d\mathcal E/dx=\rho/\varepsilon\); \(np=n_i^2e^{(F_n-F_p)/kT}\).
