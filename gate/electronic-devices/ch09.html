9.1 Junction FET (JFET)

An n-channel JFET is a bar of n-type silicon with p\({}^+\) gates diffused on both sides; the gate junction is kept reverse biased (\(V_{GS}\le0\)), so the gate current is only the reverse leakage (\(\sim\) nA): very high input resistance. Reverse bias widens the depletion regions and narrows the channel (voltage-controlled resistor); at \(V_{GS}=V_P\) (pinch-off voltage, negative for n-channel) the channel closes.

JFET characteristics

Saturation (\(V_{DS}\ge V_{GS}-V_P\), \(V_P\le V_{GS}\le0\)): \(\ I_D=I_{DSS}\left(1-\dfrac{V_{GS}}{V_P}\right)^2\).
Triode: \(I_D\approx\dfrac{2I_{DSS}}{V_P^2}\left[(V_{GS}-V_P)V_{DS}-\tfrac12V_{DS}^2\right]\).
\(g_m=\dfrac{2I_{DSS}}{|V_P|}\left(1-\dfrac{V_{GS}}{V_P}\right)=g_{m0}\left(1-\dfrac{V_{GS}}{V_P}\right)=\dfrac{2\sqrt{I_{DSS}I_D}}{|V_P|}\).
\(I_{DSS}\) is the saturation current at \(V_{GS}=0\). Pinch-off voltage \(|V_P|=\dfrac{qN_Da^2}{2\varepsilon_s}\) for channel half-width \(a\).
Self-bias: \(V_{GS}=-I_DR_S\) (gate at ground through \(R_G\)).

Solved Example

\(I_{DSS}=8\) mA, \(V_P=-4\) V: at \(V_{GS}=-2\) V, \(I_D=8(1-0.5)^2=2\) mA; \(g_{m0}=2\times8/4=4\) mS and \(g_m=4\times0.5=2\) mS.

JFET vs MOSFET. JFET has a depletion-mode channel, \(V_{GS}\le0\) for n-channel, input impedance \(\sim10^9\,\Omega\) (MOSFET \(>10^{12}\,\Omega\)), lower noise (1/\(f\)) and is used in low-noise front ends and analog switches; MOSFETs dominate ICs. MESFET (Schottky gate, GaAs) uses the same equations.

9.2 MOSFET small-signal model

Low-frequency model

\(i_d=g_mv_{gs}+g_{mb}v_{bs}+\dfrac{v_{ds}}{r_o}\).
\(g_m=\dfrac{2I_D}{V_{ov}}\), \(\ r_o=\dfrac1{\lambda I_D}\), \(\ g_{mb}=\chi g_m\).
Common-source amplifier: \(A_v=-g_m(r_o\parallel R_D\parallel R_L)\); source follower \(A_v\approx\dfrac{g_mR_S}{1+g_mR_S}\); common-gate \(A_v=g_m(R_D\parallel R_L)\).
High-frequency capacitances (saturation): \(C_{gs}\approx\dfrac23WLC_{ox}+C_{ov}\), \(C_{gd}=C_{ov}\) (overlap).
Unity-gain frequency: \(f_T=\dfrac{g_m}{2\pi(C_{gs}+C_{gd})}\approx\dfrac{3\mu_nV_{ov}}{4\pi L^2}\).

Solved Example

\(g_m=2\) mS, \(R_D=5\,\)k\(\Omega\), \(r_o=50\,\)k\(\Omega\): \(A_v=-2\,\text{mS}\times(5\parallel50)\text{k}=-9.1\).
\(\mu_n=400\,\text{cm}^2/\text{V\,s}\), \(V_{ov}=0.2\) V, \(L=0.1\,\mu\)m \(=10^{-5}\) cm: \(f_T=\dfrac{3\times400\times0.2}{4\pi\times10^{-10}}=1.9\times10^{11}\) Hz \(=191\) GHz.

9.3 CMOS fundamentals

CMOS places an n-MOSFET and a p-MOSFET together on one die; the p-device sits in an n-well (or the n-device in a p-well, or twin-well). In an inverter the gates are tied together (input) and the drains tied together (output): \(V_{in}=0\) turns the PMOS on, \(V_{in}=V_{DD}\) turns the NMOS on. Only during switching does current flow from \(V_{DD}\) to ground, so static power is nearly zero.

  • VTC regions: (i) NMOS off, PMOS linear; (ii) NMOS sat, PMOS linear; (iii) both saturated (the sharp transition, gain \(\gg1\)); (iv) NMOS linear, PMOS sat; (v) PMOS off. \(V_M=\dfrac{V_{Tn}+r(V_{DD}-|V_{Tp}|)}{1+r}\) (Ch. 10 of the digital notes).
  • Latch-up: the parasitic pnpn structure formed by the well and substrate can turn on like a thyristor and short \(V_{DD}\) to ground. Remedies: guard rings, well taps, retrograde wells, epitaxial substrate.
  • The complementary pull-up/pull-down gives rail-to-rail swing and good noise margins; PMOS is sized \(\approx2\)–\(3\times\) wider to equalise \(t_{pLH}\) and \(t_{pHL}\).

Constant-field scaling keeps the field, and hence reliability and power density, unchanged; supply voltage is limited in practice by \(V_T\) and sub-threshold leakage, so real scaling lies in between (generalised scaling).

9.4.1 Short-channel effects

  • \(V_T\) roll-off and DIBL (drain-induced barrier lowering): \(V_T\) falls as \(L\) shrinks or \(V_{DS}\) rises.
  • Velocity saturation: \(v\to v_{sat}\) at high fields, so \(I_D\propto W C_{ox}v_{sat}V_{ov}\) (linear in \(V_{ov}\) instead of quadratic) and \(g_m\) saturates.
  • Punch-through, hot-carrier degradation, gate-oxide tunnelling leakage, increasing off-state leakage.
  • Mitigation: lightly doped drains (LDD), halo implants, high-\(k\)/metal gates, strained silicon, SOI and FinFET/multigate devices (better electrostatic control and swing).
Conceptual Trap

(i) JFET gate must be reverse biased; forward bias destroys the high input resistance. (ii) For the JFET \(V_P\) is negative (n-channel) so \((1-V_{GS}/V_P)\) is between 0 and 1. (iii) Under constant-field scaling power density stays constant, not power per gate. (iv) \(g_m\) of a JFET is not \(2I_D/V_{ov}\) with \(V_T\); use \(V_P\). (v) CMOS draws large current only during transitions.

GATE Shortcut

JFET: first find \(I_D\) with \((1-V_{GS}/V_P)^2\), then \(g_m=2\sqrt{I_{DSS}I_D}/|V_P|\). Scaling by \(S\): delay \(\div S\), power per gate \(\div S^2\), density constant. \(f_T\propto\mu V_{ov}/L^2\).

9.5 GATE problems

GATE 1-Mark Problems

Q1. In an n-channel JFET the gate–source junction is normally (A) forward biased (B) reverse biased (C) unbiased (D) in breakdown.

Q2. Under constant-field scaling by \(S\) the power density (A) increases by \(S\) (B) increases by \(S^2\) (C) is unchanged (D) decreases by \(S\).

Q3. Velocity saturation makes \(I_D\) in saturation proportional to (A) \(V_{ov}^2\) (B) \(V_{ov}\) (C) \(V_{ov}^{1/2}\) (D) \(V_{ov}^3\).

Q4. Latch-up in CMOS is caused by (A) a parasitic pnpn thyristor (B) gate leakage (C) channel-length modulation (D) body effect.

Answers: Q1 (B). Q2 (C). Q3 (B). Q4 (A).

GATE 2-Mark Problems

Q5. \(I_{DSS}=8\) mA, \(V_P=-4\) V, \(V_{GS}=-2\) V. \(I_D\) and \(g_m\) are (A) 2 mA, 2 mS (B) 4 mA, 2 mS (C) 2 mA, 4 mS (D) 1 mA, 1 mS.

Q6. A common-source stage has \(g_m=2\) mS, \(R_D=5\,\)k, \(r_o=50\,\)k. The gain magnitude is (A) 10 (B) 9.1 (C) 100 (D) 5.

Q7. A device scaled by \(S=4\) (constant-field) has gate delay (A) 4 times longer (B) 4 times shorter (C) 16 times shorter (D) unchanged.

Answers: Q5 (A). Q6 (B). Q7 (B).

Numerical Answer Type (NAT)

Q8. \(I_D\) (mA, two decimals) of a JFET with \(I_{DSS}=10\) mA, \(V_P=-5\) V at \(V_{GS}=-1\) V: .

Q9. Power per gate after constant-field scaling by \(S=4\), relative to the original: (as a fraction, e.g. 0.25).

Answers: Q8: \(10(1-0.2)^2=6.40\). Q9: \(1/16=0.0625\).

Multiple Select (MSQ)

Q10. Select the true statements. (A) JFET has a very high input resistance. (B) DIBL lowers \(V_T\) at high \(V_{DS}\). (C) Constant-voltage scaling increases the electric field. (D) CMOS dissipates large static power.

Answers: Q10: (A), (B), (C).

Chapter Test

T1. Pinch-off voltage sign for n-JFET.   T2. Name two short-channel effects.   T3. \(g_{m0}\) for \(I_{DSS}=6\) mA, \(V_P=-3\) V.

Answers: T1: negative. T2: DIBL, velocity saturation. T3: 4 mS.

One-Page Revision
  • JFET \(I_D=I_{DSS}(1-V_{GS}/V_P)^2\); \(g_{m0}=2I_{DSS}/|V_P|\); reverse-biased gate.
  • MOS small signal \(g_m,r_o,g_{mb}\); \(f_T\approx3\mu V_{ov}/(4\pi L^2)\).
  • CMOS: complementary, no static current, latch-up risk.
  • Scaling \(S\): delay \(1/S\), power \(1/S^2\), density constant; short-channel: DIBL, velocity saturation.
