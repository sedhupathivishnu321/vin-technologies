GATE Focus

Qualitative process flow (crystal growth, oxidation, diffusion, implantation, lithography, etching, deposition) with a few standard calculations: oxide thickness, Gaussian diffusion profile, implant dose and peak concentration, lithographic resolution and yield.

10.1 Silicon wafer preparation

Electronic-grade polysilicon is melted and a single crystal is pulled from the melt by the Czochralski (CZ) method (seed crystal rotated and slowly withdrawn; dopant added to the melt; oxygen from the quartz crucible is incorporated). The float-zone (FZ) method avoids a crucible and gives purer, high-resistivity silicon. The ingot is ground, sliced, lapped and chemically-mechanically polished to a mirror finish. Crystal orientation \(\langle100\rangle\) is used for MOS (low interface-state density), \(\langle111\rangle\) for bipolar. The segregation coefficient \(k_0=C_s/C_l\) causes the dopant concentration to vary along the ingot.

10.2 Epitaxy

A thin single-crystal layer grown on the wafer with its own doping, by chemical vapour deposition from SiCl\(_4\)/SiH\(_2\)Cl\(_2\) with H\(_2\) at \(\sim1000\)–\(1200^\circ\)C (VPE) or by MBE. Used for lightly doped layers above heavy substrates (reduces latch-up in CMOS, forms the collector in bipolar ICs). Heteroepitaxy (SiGe, GaAs on Si) requires lattice matching or strain management.

10.3 Thermal oxidation

SiO\(_2\) is grown by exposing silicon to O\(_2\) (dry, slow, high quality: gate oxide) or H\(_2\)O vapour (wet, fast: field oxide) at 900–1200\({}^\circ\)C. About 44% of the final oxide thickness comes from consumed silicon.

Deal–Grove model

\(x_{ox}^2+A\,x_{ox}=B\,(t+\tau)\).
Thin oxide (linear, reaction limited): \(x_{ox}\approx\dfrac BA(t+\tau)\). Thick oxide (parabolic, diffusion limited): \(x_{ox}\approx\sqrt{Bt}\).
Silicon consumed \(=0.44\,x_{ox}\). Oxidation rate rises with temperature, with steam, and with high-pressure oxidation; \(\langle111\rangle\) oxidises faster than \(\langle100\rangle\).

Solved Example

Wet oxidation with \(B=0.5\,\mu\text{m}^2\)/h for 1 h (thick limit): \(x_{ox}=\sqrt{0.5}=0.71\,\mu\)m; silicon consumed \(=0.31\,\mu\)m. Doubling the time raises the thickness only by \(\sqrt2\).

Local oxidation (LOCOS) and shallow-trench isolation (STI) separate devices; the oxide is also a mask against diffusion and a dielectric. Dopant redistribution at the Si–SiO\(_2\) interface (boron depletes, phosphorus piles up) matters for well and channel profiles.

10.4 Diffusion

Dopants move by diffusion at high temperature (\(900\)–\(1100^\circ\)C) following Fick’s laws with \(D=D_0e^{-E_a/kT}\).

Diffusion profiles

Constant (infinite) source: \(N(x,t)=N_s\,\text{erfc}\!\left(\dfrac{x}{2\sqrt{Dt}}\right)\); surface concentration fixed at the solid solubility.
Limited source (drive-in): \(N(x,t)=\dfrac{Q}{\sqrt{\pi Dt}}\exp\!\left(-\dfrac{x^2}{4Dt}\right)\), \(\ N_s=\dfrac{Q}{\sqrt{\pi Dt}}\) falls with time.
Junction depth: \(N(x_j)=N_B\) (background doping). Characteristic length \(2\sqrt{Dt}\).

Drive-in

\(Q=10^{14}\,\text{cm}^{-2}\), \(D=10^{-13}\,\text{cm}^2/\)s, \(t=1\) h: \(Dt=3.6\times10^{-10}\,\text{cm}^2\), \(N_s=\dfrac{10^{14}}{\sqrt{\pi\times3.6\times10^{-10}}}=2.97\times10^{18}\,\text{cm}^{-3}\). For \(N_B=10^{16}\): \(x_j=\sqrt{4Dt\ln(N_s/N_B)}=\sqrt{4(3.6\times10^{-10})\ln297}=0.91\,\mu\)m.

10.5 Ion implantation

Ionised dopants are accelerated (10 keV–MeV) into the wafer. Advantages: precise dose and depth control, low temperature, masking by resist. Disadvantages: lattice damage (annealing needed to repair and activate), channelling (reduced by tilting the wafer \(\sim7^\circ\)).

Implant

Dose \(Q=\dfrac{I\,t}{qA}\) (ions/cm\({}^2\)).
Profile: \(N(x)=\dfrac{Q}{\sqrt{2\pi}\,\Delta R_p}\exp\!\left[-\dfrac{(x-R_p)^2}{2\Delta R_p^2}\right]\), peak \(N_p=\dfrac{Q}{\sqrt{2\pi}\Delta R_p}\approx\dfrac{0.4\,Q}{\Delta R_p}\) at the projected range \(R_p\) (increases with energy).

Solved Example

\(Q=10^{14}\,\text{cm}^{-2}\), \(\Delta R_p=0.05\,\mu\text{m}=5\times10^{-6}\) cm: \(N_p=0.4\times10^{14}/5\times10^{-6}=8\times10^{18}\,\text{cm}^{-3}\). A beam of \(1\,\mu\)A over \(50\) cm\({}^2\) for \(100\) s delivers \(\dfrac{10^{-6}\times100}{1.6\times10^{-19}\times50}=1.25\times10^{13}\,\text{cm}^{-2}\).

10.6 Photolithography

A photoresist film is spin-coated, soft-baked, exposed through a mask or reticle, developed, hard-baked and later stripped. Positive resist: exposed regions dissolve; negative: exposed regions remain.

Resolution and depth of focus

Minimum feature (Rayleigh): \(R=k_1\dfrac{\lambda}{NA}\). Depth of focus: \(DOF=k_2\dfrac{\lambda}{NA^2}\).
Example: \(\lambda=193\) nm, \(NA=1.35\) (immersion), \(k_1=0.3\): \(R=43\) nm; \(k_2=0.5\): \(DOF=53\) nm. Smaller \(\lambda\) and larger \(NA\) improve resolution but reduce DOF. EUV (\(13.5\) nm) is the next step.

Contact/proximity printing wears masks and diffracts; projection printing (step-and-repeat) is standard. Resolution enhancement: phase-shift masks, OPC, multiple patterning.

10.7 Etching, deposition and metallisation

  • Etching: wet (isotropic, selective) vs dry/plasma RIE (anisotropic). Etch selectivity and undercut matter.
  • Deposition: CVD (LPCVD poly-Si, Si\(_3\)N\(_4\); PECVD at low temperature for dielectrics), PVD (sputtering of Al, Ti, W), ALD for conformal high-\(k\) films.
  • Metallisation: Al (with Cu/Si) historically; Cu damascene with barrier layers in modern back-ends; silicides (TiSi\(_2\), CoSi\(_2\), NiSi) lower contact resistance; low-\(k\) dielectrics reduce interconnect delay (RC).
  • CMP planarises layers before the next level.

10.8 Process flow of a CMOS inverter (twin-well, self-aligned gate)

Substrate/epi \(\to\) well implants and drive-in \(\to\) STI isolation \(\to\) gate oxide growth \(\to\) poly-Si deposition and gate patterning \(\to\) LDD implants \(\to\) spacer formation \(\to\) source/drain implants and anneal \(\to\) silicide \(\to\) contacts and multilayer metal \(\to\) passivation \(\to\) test, dicing, packaging. Self-aligned gate: the poly-Si gate masks the source/drain implant, so the channel is aligned to the gate with minimal overlap capacitance.

10.9 Yield

Poisson model: \(Y=e^{-AD_0}\) (\(A\) die area, \(D_0\) defect density). For \(A=1\,\text{cm}^2\) and \(D_0=0.5\,\text{cm}^{-2}\): \(Y=e^{-0.5}=60.7\%\). Smaller dice improve yield.

Conceptual Trap

(i) Diffusion from a limited source decreases the surface concentration with time; constant-source diffusion keeps \(N_s\) fixed. (ii) Implanted profiles are peaked below the surface (at \(R_p\)), diffused profiles are maximum at the surface. (iii) Better resolution (small \(R\)) costs depth of focus. (iv) Oxidation consumes silicon (44%). (v) Implantation damage must be annealed.

GATE Shortcut

Thick oxide: \(x\propto\sqrt t\); \(x_j=2\sqrt{Dt}\,\sqrt{\ln(N_s/N_B)}\) for Gaussian. Peak implant \(=0.4Q/\Delta R_p\). \(Y=e^{-AD_0}\).

10.10 GATE problems

GATE 1-Mark Problems

Q1. A thin single crystal grown on a substrate is called (A) diffusion layer (B) epitaxial layer (C) implanted layer (D) oxide.

Q2. The doping profile peak of an ion-implanted layer lies (A) at the surface (B) at the projected range \(R_p\) (C) at the junction (D) at the substrate.

Q3. Which method gives the most precise control of dose? (A) diffusion (B) ion implantation (C) epitaxy (D) alloying.

Q4. In thick-oxide growth, thickness varies as (A) \(t\) (B) \(\sqrt t\) (C) \(t^2\) (D) \(\ln t\).

Answers: Q1 (B). Q2 (B). Q3 (B). Q4 (B).

Q5. Silicon consumed in growing 0.7 \(\mu\)m of oxide is approximately (A) 0.31 \(\mu\)m (B) 0.7 \(\mu\)m (C) 0.44 \(\mu\)m (D) 1.6 \(\mu\)m.

Q6. Limited-source diffusion with \(Q=10^{14}\), \(D=10^{-13}\), \(t=1\) h. The surface concentration (cm\({}^{-3}\)) is (A) \(3\times10^{18}\) (B) \(3\times10^{16}\) (C) \(10^{14}\) (D) \(5\times10^{19}\).

Q7. \(\lambda=193\) nm, \(NA=1.35\), \(k_1=0.3\). The minimum feature is about (A) 43 nm (B) 100 nm (C) 22 nm (D) 193 nm.

Answers: Q5 (A). Q6 (A). Q7 (A).

Multiple Select (MSQ)

Answers: Q11: (A), (B), (C).

Chapter Test

T1. Self-aligned gate advantage.   T2. Dry vs wet oxidation uses.   T3. Formula for resolution.

Answers: T1: no gate–source/drain misalignment, low overlap capacitance. T2: dry for gate oxide, wet for thick field oxide. T3: \(R=k_1\lambda/NA\).
