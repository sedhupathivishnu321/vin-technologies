Chapter 1

English Grammar and Vocabulary

In this chapter
  1. 1.1 Basic English grammar
  2. 1.2 Vocabulary and word usage
  3. 1.3 GATE problems
GATE Focus

Subject–verb agreement, pronoun and preposition usage, tense and modifier errors, and word-pair vocabulary (synonyms, antonyms, confusables, idioms). GATE tests recognition of the correct form, so learn the rules as quick filters and eliminate options.

1.1 Basic English grammar

1.1.1 Subject–verb agreement

Agreement rules

Singular subjects: each, every, either, neither, one, anyone, everyone, nobody, much (“Each of the students has…”, “Neither of the plans is…”).
“One of the …” takes a plural noun but a singular verb: “One of my friends is an engineer.”
Either… or / neither… nor: the verb agrees with the nearer subject: “Neither the manager nor the employees were present.”
Together with / along with / as well as do not change the number: “The captain, along with his team, is here.”
Collective nouns (team, committee, jury) are singular when acting as one unit, plural when members act separately. Plural-form singulars: news, physics, mathematics, economics take singular verbs. A quantity or time period as a unit is singular: “Ten kilometres is a long way.”

1.1.2 Tenses, articles and pronouns

Quick rules

Present perfect (has/have + V3) links the past to the present; use for with a period and since with a starting point: “She has lived here for five years / since 2019.” Past perfect (had + V3) marks the earlier of two past events.
Articles: a before a consonant sound (a university, a one-way street), an before a vowel sound (an hour, an MBA); the for a specific or unique noun; no article for general plurals and uncountables.
Pronouns: who (subject), whom (object): “To whom did you write?”; after prepositions use object case: “between you and me”, not “between you and I”. its (possessive) vs. it’s (it is). Each other is for two, one another for more than two.

1.1.3 Prepositions, modifiers and parallelism

Frequently tested usage

Prepositions: senior/junior/superior/inferior to, prefer to, different from, capable of, comprise (no “of”), discuss (no “about”), good at, interested in, responsible for, married to.
Less / fewer: fewer for countable plurals (fewer students), less for uncountables (less water). Between / among: between two, among more than two.
Parallelism: keep list items in the same form: “He likes reading, writing and swimming” (not “to swim”).
Dangling modifier: “Walking to the lab, the rain started” is wrong; “Walking to the lab, I was caught in the rain” is right.

Conditionals and voice. Real: “If it rains, we stay.” Unreal present: “If I were you, I would go.” Passive: object becomes subject, be + V3 (“The bridge was built by them”).

1.2 Vocabulary and word usage

1.2.1 Building blocks

Prefixes: un-, in-, im-, dis-, non- negate; pre- before; post- after; mis- wrongly; anti- against. Suffixes: -able (can be done), -less (without), -ful (full of), -ology (study of). Roots: bene (good) \(\to\) benevolent; mal (bad) \(\to\) malevolent; chron (time) \(\to\) chronic; vor (eat) \(\to\) carnivore.

1.2.2 Synonyms, antonyms and confusables

Commonly confused pairs

affect (verb: influence) / effect (noun: result); principal (head; main) / principle (rule); complement (completes) / compliment (praise); stationary (still) / stationery (paper); council (body) / counsel (advice); eminent (distinguished) / imminent (about to happen); lose / loose; accept (take) / except (exclude); disinterested (impartial) / uninterested (not interested).

Idioms. Burn the midnight oil: work late at night. Break the ice: start a conversation. Hit the nail on the head: be exactly right. A blessing in disguise: a good thing that seemed bad. Once in a blue moon: very rarely. Bite the bullet: face something unpleasant bravely.

Conceptual Trap

(i) A prepositional phrase between subject and verb does not change agreement (“The box of tools is…”). (ii) “Between you and I” is wrong. (iii) “Discuss about” and “comprise of” are wrong. (iv) Affect and effect swap easily; test by substituting “influence” or “result”. (v) “Fewer” with uncountables is wrong.

GATE Shortcut

Strip the sentence to subject + verb, then check number. For prepositions, recall the fixed phrase. For vocabulary, guess from the root or from the word’s positive/negative “charge” (e.g. mal- is negative). Eliminate options that change the tense of the rest of the sentence.

1.3 GATE problems

GATE 1-Mark Problems

Q1. Neither of the engineers the answer. (A) know (B) knows (C) have known (D) are knowing.

Q2. She is senior me in the department. (A) than (B) to (C) from (D) over.

Q3. The word closest in meaning to ephemeral is (A) lasting (B) short-lived (C) harmful (D) ancient.

Q4. The word opposite in meaning to benevolent is (A) kind (B) malevolent (C) generous (D) gentle.

Answers: Q1 (B). Q2 (B). Q3 (B). Q4 (B).

GATE 2-Mark Problems

Q5. Which sentence is grammatically correct? (A) Each of the students have submitted their report. (B) The team of designers are meeting the client. (C) One of my friends is an engineer. (D) Either of the two plans are acceptable.

Q6. The idiom burn the midnight oil means (A) waste fuel (B) work late into the night (C) start a fire (D) go to bed early.

Q7. Choose the pair that fills the blanks: “The new rule will adversely small businesses, and its will be felt for years.” (A) effect, affect (B) affect, effects (C) affect, affects (D) effect, effects.

Answers: Q5 (C): “one” takes a singular verb; the others have agreement errors. Q6 (B). Q7 (B): affect is the verb, effects the noun.

Numerical Answer Type (NAT)

Q8. Of the sentences (i) He don’t like coffee. (ii) Neither the manager nor the employees were present. (iii) Between you and I, it is wrong. (iv) Fewer students came today. (v) She has lived here since five years, the number that are grammatically correct is .

Q9. The sentence “Though he was tired, he continued working because the deadline was near” contains clauses.

Q10. Among unhappy, uncle, under, unfold, unit, the number of words in which un- is a negating or reversing prefix is .

Answers: Q8: 2, namely (ii) and (iv). Q9: 3. Q10: 2 (unhappy, unfold).

Multiple Select (MSQ)

Q11. Which are correct? (A) fewer than ten items (B) less than ten items (C) between the two brothers (D) among the two brothers.

Answers: Q11: (A), (C).

Chapter Test

T1. Correct the error: “The news are good.”   T2. Meaning of frugal?   T3. Principal or principle: “the school announced the new .”

Answers: T1: “The news is good.” T2: thrifty. T3: principal, principle.

One-Page Revision
  • Each, every, either, neither, one \(\to\) singular; “one of the” + plural noun + singular verb; nearest-subject rule for either/or.
  • For (period) vs. since (starting point); between you and me; fewer (count) vs. less (mass).
  • Fixed prepositions: senior to, prefer to, different from, discuss (no about).
  • Roots/prefixes unlock words: bene- good, mal- bad; confusables affect/effect, principal/principle.
