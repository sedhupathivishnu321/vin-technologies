Ratios, Proportions and Percentages

GATE Focus

Ratio manipulation, direct and inverse proportion, alligation and mixtures, percentage change (including successive changes), profit and loss, simple and compound interest, and averages. These are the most frequent word problems in the GA section.

3.1 Ratios and proportions

Ratio and proportion

\(a:b=\frac ab\). If \(a:b=2:3\) and \(b:c=4:5\), make the common term equal: \(a:b:c=8:12:15\).
Proportion \(a:b=c:d\iff ad=bc\). Componendo–dividendo: \(\frac ab=\frac cd\Rightarrow\frac{a+b}{a-b}=\frac{c+d}{c-d}\).
Direct variation \(y\propto x\): \(y/x\) constant. Inverse variation \(y\propto1/x\): \(xy\) constant (more workers \(\to\) fewer days).
Partnership: profit is shared in the ratio of (capital \(\times\) time).
Dividing a quantity in ratio \(a:b\): shares \(\frac{a}{a+b}\) and \(\frac{b}{a+b}\) of the total.

3.1.1 Alligation and mixtures

Rule of alligation

Mixing two ingredients of strengths \(c_1<c_2\) to get a mean strength \(m\): \(\dfrac{\text{quantity of cheaper}}{\text{quantity of dearer}}=\dfrac{c_2-m}{m-c_1}\).
Replacement: after removing and replacing a fraction \(f\) of a mixture \(n\) times, the original ingredient left is \((1-f)^n\) of the original quantity.

Solved Example

To get a \(52\%\) solution from \(40\%\) and \(60\%\) solutions: ratio \(=(60-52):(52-40)=8:12=2:3\) of the \(40\%\) to the \(60\%\) solution. A \(60\,\)kg alloy has copper:zinc \(=2:3\) (copper \(24\,\)kg, zinc \(36\,\)kg). To make the ratio \(1:1\) add \(12\,\)kg of copper.

3.2 Percentages

Percentage rules

\(x\%\) of \(N=\frac{x}{100}N\). Percentage change \(=\frac{\text{new}-\text{old}}{\text{old}}\times100\).
Successive changes multiply: increases of \(a\%\) and \(b\%\) give net \(\left[(1+\frac a{100})(1+\frac b{100})-1\right]\times100=a+b+\frac{ab}{100}\) per cent. An increase of \(20\%\) then a decrease of \(20\%\): \(1.2\times0.8=0.96\), a net \(4\%\) fall.
To undo an increase of \(r\%\) you need a decrease of \(\frac{r}{100+r}\times100\%\) (e.g. \(25\%\) rise needs a \(20\%\) fall).
Population/growth: \(P_n=P_0(1+r)^n\).

Useful fractions: \(\frac12=50\%\), \(\frac13=33.\overline3\%\), \(\frac14=25\%\), \(\frac15=20\%\), \(\frac16=16.\overline6\%\), \(\frac18=12.5\%\), \(\frac19=11.\overline1\%\), \(\frac1{12}=8.\overline3\%\).

3.3 Profit, loss and discount

Commercial arithmetic

Profit \(=\mathrm{SP}-\mathrm{CP}\); profit% \(=\frac{\text{profit}}{\mathrm{CP}}\times100\); \(\mathrm{SP}=\mathrm{CP}(1\pm\frac{p}{100})\). Always take profit/loss on cost price.
Discount is on marked price (MP): \(\mathrm{SP}=\mathrm{MP}(1-\frac d{100})\). Two successive discounts \(d_1,d_2\) combine as \(d_1+d_2-\frac{d_1d_2}{100}\).
Simple interest \(\mathrm{SI}=\frac{PRT}{100}\). Compound interest \(A=P(1+\frac R{100})^T\), \(\mathrm{CI}=A-P\). For \(2\) years \(\mathrm{CI}-\mathrm{SI}=P\left(\frac{R}{100}\right)^2\).

Solved Example

Cost \(450\), desired profit \(20\%\) after a \(10\%\) discount: \(\mathrm{SP}=540\), \(\mathrm{MP}=540/0.9=600\). CI on \(10\,000\) at \(10\%\) for \(2\) years: \(10\,000(1.1^2-1)=2100\). SI on \(5000\) at \(8\%\) for \(3\) years: \(1200\).

3.4 Averages

\(\text{Average}=\frac{\text{sum}}{n}\). Weighted average \(=\frac{\sum w_ix_i}{\sum w_i}\). If a new member changes the mean, the change \(\times\) new count equals (new value \(-\) old mean). Average speed over equal distances is the harmonic mean (Chapter 7).

Conceptual Trap

(i) A \(20\%\) increase followed by a \(20\%\) decrease does not return to the start. (ii) Profit is on cost price, discount on marked price. (iii) “\(A\) is \(25\%\) more than \(B\)” means \(A=1.25B\), so \(B\) is \(20\%\) less than \(A\). (iv) CI after \(n\) years is \(P[(1+r)^n-1]\), not \(PRT\). (v) In alligation put the difference with the opposite end on each side.

GATE Shortcut

Use multipliers: \(+10\%\to1.1\), \(-20\%\to0.8\). For ratios assume a convenient total (e.g. 100). For successive \(+a\) and \(+b\): \(a+b+ab/100\). Check CI against SI first: CI \(>\) SI for \(T>1\).

3.5 GATE problems

GATE 1-Mark Problems

Q1. If \(A:B=2:3\) and \(B:C=4:5\), then \(A:B:C=\) (A) 2:3:5 (B) 8:12:15 (C) 8:12:5 (D) 2:6:5.

Q2. \(25\%\) of \(80\) is (A) 10 (B) 20 (C) 25 (D) 32.

Q3. A price is increased by \(20\%\) and then decreased by \(20\%\). The net change is (A) 0 (B) \(4\%\) decrease (C) \(4\%\) increase (D) \(2\%\) decrease.

Q4. If \(y\) varies inversely with \(x\) and \(y=6\) when \(x=4\), then \(y\) when \(x=8\) is (A) 3 (B) 12 (C) 2 (D) 24.

Answers: Q1 (B). Q2 (B). Q3 (B). Q4 (A): \(xy=24\).

GATE 2-Mark Problems

Q5. In what ratio must \(40\%\) and \(60\%\) solutions be mixed to get a \(52\%\) solution? (A) 2:3 (B) 3:2 (C) 1:2 (D) 4:3.

Q6. An article costing \(450\) is to be sold at \(20\%\) profit after allowing a \(10\%\) discount. The marked price should be (A) 540 (B) 600 (C) 650 (D) 500.

Q7. The compound interest on \(10\,000\) at \(10\%\) per annum for \(2\) years is (A) 2000 (B) 2100 (C) 2200 (D) 2310.

Answers: Q5 (A). Q6 (B). Q7 (B): \(1.21\times10\,000-10\,000\).

Numerical Answer Type (NAT)

Q8. A population grows by \(10\%\) in one year and \(20\%\) in the next. The total percentage growth is .

Q9. Kilograms of copper to add to a \(60\,\)kg alloy (copper:zinc \(=2:3\)) to make the ratio \(1:1\): .

Q10. Simple interest on \(5000\) at \(8\%\) per annum for \(3\) years: .

Answers: Q8: \(1.1\times1.2=1.32\), so \(32\). Q9: copper \(24\), zinc \(36\); add \(12\). Q10: \(1200\).

Multiple Select (MSQ)

Q11. Which are true? (A) A \(25\%\) rise needs a \(20\%\) fall to restore the original. (B) CI equals SI for \(2\) years. (C) Two successive \(10\%\) discounts equal a \(19\%\) discount. (D) A \(20\%\) rise followed by a \(20\%\) fall leaves the value unchanged.

Answers: Q11: (A), (C).

Chapter Test

T1. \(15\%\) of \(200\)?   T2. Share of the smaller part when \(90\) is divided \(2:3\)?   T3. Net effect of \(+10\%\) then \(+10\%\)?

Answers: T1: 30. T2: 36. T3: \(21\%\).
