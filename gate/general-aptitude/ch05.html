Chapter 5

Permutations, Combinations and Probability

In this chapter
  1. 5.1 Counting principles
  2. 5.2 Probability
  3. 5.3 GATE problems
GATE Focus

Counting arrangements and selections (with and without repetition, circular arrangements, committees), and basic probability with dice, coins and cards, including conditional and “at least one” events.

5.1 Counting principles

Fundamental rules

Multiplication: if a task has independent stages with \(m\) and \(n\) ways, the total is \(mn\). Addition: if stages are alternatives (either/or), add.
\(n!=n(n-1)\cdots1\), \(0!=1\). \(\ {}^nP_r=\dfrac{n!}{(n-r)!}\) (order matters). \(\ {}^nC_r=\dfrac{n!}{r!(n-r)!}\) (order does not matter), \({}^nC_r=\,{}^nC_{n-r}\), \({}^nC_r+\,{}^nC_{r-1}=\,{}^{n+1}C_r\).
Arrangements of \(n\) items with repeats \(p,q,r\) of the same kind: \(\dfrac{n!}{p!q!r!}\).
Circular arrangements of \(n\) distinct items: \((n-1)!\) (clockwise/anticlockwise distinct), \(\frac{(n-1)!}2\) if reflections are the same (necklace).
Handshakes among \(n\) people: \({}^nC_2\). Diagonals of an \(n\)-gon: \({}^nC_2-n\).

Solved Example

Arrangements of LEVEL: \(\frac{5!}{2!\,2!}=30\). MISSISSIPPI: \(\frac{11!}{4!\,4!\,2!}=34650\). A committee of \(3\) from \(5\) men and \(4\) women with at least one woman: total \({}^9C_3=84\) minus all men \({}^5C_3=10\) gives \(74\).

5.1.1 Typical constraints

Items together: glue them into one block, then multiply by the internal arrangements (A and B adjacent among \(n\) in a row: \(2\cdot(n-1)!\)). Items never together: total \(-\) together. Digits numbers: choose position by position, handling restrictions (e.g. leading digit nonzero) first. Distribution: \(n\) identical items into \(r\) boxes (each box \(\ge0\)): \({}^{n+r-1}C_{r-1}\).

5.2 Probability

Rules

\(P(A)=\dfrac{\text{favourable}}{\text{total}}\) for equally likely outcomes; \(0\le P\le1\). \(P(\overline A)=1-P(A)\); \(P(A\cup B)=P(A)+P(B)-P(A\cap B)\).
Conditional: \(P(A|B)=\frac{P(A\cap B)}{P(B)}\). Independent events: \(P(A\cap B)=P(A)P(B)\).
At least one \(=1-P(\text{none})\). Binomial: \(P(k\text{ successes in }n)=\,{}^nC_kp^k(1-p)^{n-k}\). Expected value \(E=\sum xP(x)\).
Standard spaces: coin \(2\); die \(6\); two dice \(36\) (sum \(7\) in \(6\) ways); deck \(52\) (\(4\) suits \(\times\) \(13\); \(4\) aces, \(12\) face cards, \(26\) red).

Solved Example

Two aces drawn without replacement: \(\frac4{52}\cdot\frac3{51}=\frac1{221}\). At least one head in \(3\) tosses: \(1-\frac18=\frac78\). Two dice, sum \(7\): \(\frac16\).

Conceptual Trap

(i) Permutation vs. combination: order matters \(\Rightarrow\) \(P\). (ii) Forgetting to divide by \(p!\) for repeated letters. (iii) Drawing without replacement changes the second probability. (iv) Mutually exclusive \(\ne\) independent. (v) Circular arrangements use \((n-1)!\), not \(n!\).

GATE Shortcut

For “at least” use the complement. For committees use \({}^nC_r\) with the constraint as a subtraction. For probability with dice, list the \(36\) outcomes. For small \(r\): \({}^nC_2=\frac{n(n-1)}2\), \({}^nC_3=\frac{n(n-1)(n-2)}6\).

5.3 GATE problems

GATE 1-Mark Problems

Q1. \({}^5P_2=\) (A) 10 (B) 20 (C) 60 (D) 120.

Q2. The number of distinct arrangements of the letters of LEVEL is (A) 120 (B) 60 (C) 30 (D) 24.

Q3. The probability that the sum of two fair dice is \(7\) is (A) \(1/12\) (B) \(1/9\) (C) \(1/6\) (D) \(5/36\).

Q4. \({}^8C_3=\) (A) 24 (B) 56 (C) 336 (D) 120.

Answers: Q1 (B). Q2 (C). Q3 (C). Q4 (B).

GATE 2-Mark Problems

Q5. A committee of \(3\) is chosen from \(5\) men and \(4\) women with at least one woman. The number of ways is (A) 84 (B) 74 (C) 64 (D) 94.

Q6. Two cards are drawn without replacement from a deck. The probability both are aces is (A) \(1/169\) (B) \(1/221\) (C) \(1/13\) (D) \(2/221\).

Q7. The number of distinct arrangements of the letters of MISSISSIPPI is (A) 34650 (B) 39916800 (C) 11!/(4!4!) (D) 69300.

Answers: Q5 (B). Q6 (B). Q7 (A).

Numerical Answer Type (NAT)

Q8. Circular arrangements of \(6\) distinct people: .

Q9. Probability (three decimals) of at least one head in \(3\) tosses of a fair coin: .

Q10. Handshakes among \(10\) people, each pair shaking once: .

Answers: Q8: \(5!=120\). Q9: \(0.875\). Q10: \({}^{10}C_2=45\).

Multiple Select (MSQ)

Q11. Which are true? (A) \({}^nC_r=\,{}^nC_{n-r}\) (B) \({}^nP_r=\,{}^nC_r\cdot r!\) (C) mutually exclusive events with positive probabilities are independent (D) \(P(\text{at least one})=1-P(\text{none})\).

Answers: Q11: (A), (B), (D).

Chapter Test

T1. Ways to arrange \(4\) books on a shelf?   T2. \(P\)(exactly one head in two tosses)?   T3. \({}^6C_2\)?

Answers: T1: 24. T2: \(1/2\). T3: 15.
