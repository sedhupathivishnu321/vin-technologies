Supplementary Syllabus Topics

State equations for networks

Choose the capacitor voltages and inductor currents as state variables; write KVL/KCL to get \(\dot{\mathbf x}=\mathbf A\mathbf x+\mathbf B\mathbf u\), \(\mathbf y=\mathbf C\mathbf x+\mathbf D\mathbf u\). The eigenvalues of \(\mathbf A\) are the poles of \(H(s)=\mathbf C(s\mathbf I-\mathbf A)^{-1}\mathbf B+\mathbf D\).

Series RLC

States \(x_1=v_C\), \(x_2=i_L\); input \(v_s\): \(\dot v_C=\dfrac{i_L}C\), \(\ \dot i_L=\dfrac{v_s-Ri_L-v_C}L\). \[\dot{\mathbf x}=\begin{bmatrix}0&\frac1C\\-\frac1L&-\frac RL\end{bmatrix}\mathbf x+\begin{bmatrix}0\\\frac1L\end{bmatrix}v_s,\qquad\det(s\mathbf I-\mathbf A)=s^2+\frac RLs+\frac1{LC}.\] For \(R=6\ \Omega\), \(L=1\) H, \(C=0.04\) F: \(\mathbf A=\begin{bmatrix}0&25\\-1&-6\end{bmatrix}\), eigenvalues \(-3\pm j4\) (underdamped, as in Chapter 10). Output \(v_C\): \(H(s)=\dfrac{25}{s^2+6s+25}\).

The number of state variables equals the number of independent energy-storage elements (minus capacitor-only loops and inductor-only cut-sets).

Interpolation of discrete-time signals

To increase the sampling rate by an integer \(L\): (i) upsample (insert \(L-1\) zeros), which compresses the spectrum and creates \(L-1\) images; (ii) low-pass filter with cutoff \(\pi/L\) and gain \(L\) to remove the images. Ideal interpolation uses \(h[n]=\sinc(n/L)\); linear interpolation uses the triangular FIR \(h=\{\frac1L,\ldots,1,\ldots,\frac1L\}\).

Solved Example

\(x=\{1,3\}\), \(L=2\): upsampled \(\{1,0,3,0\}\); filter \(h=\{0.5,1,0.5\}\): \(\{0.5,1,2,3,1.5,0\}\); after removing the one-sample delay the interpolated sequence is \(\{1,2,3,1.5,\ldots\}\): the new sample \(2\) lies midway between \(1\) and \(3\).

Decimation by \(M\): low-pass filter at \(\pi/M\) (anti-aliasing) then keep every \(M\)th sample.
