Idea

A GATE paper is 3 hours for 65 questions: roughly 2.8 minutes per question on average, but Networks/Signals questions are most efficiently solved in 1–3 minutes if you choose the right shortcut. Each section gives the trigger (what you see in the question), the shortcut, and a timed example.

36.1 Symmetry

Trigger: identical resistors on a symmetric structure; a bridge; a mirror-symmetric ladder. Shortcut: equipotential nodes can be joined; symmetric currents split equally; fold the circuit. Example (20 s): cube of \(R\): body diagonal \(5R/6\), face diagonal \(3R/4\), edge \(7R/12\). In signals: even/odd symmetry tells you which Fourier coefficients vanish and whether \(X(j\omega)\) is real.

36.2 Source transformation

Trigger: series resistor with a voltage source next to a parallel resistor with a current source. Shortcut: convert to a single form, combine sources, repeat. Example: \(12\) V in series with \(4\ \Omega\) \(=\) \(3\) A in parallel with \(4\ \Omega\); add a parallel \(2\) A source: \(5\) A in parallel with \(4\ \Omega\) \(=\) \(20\) V in series with \(4\ \Omega\).

36.3 Thevenin/Norton shortcuts

  • \(R_{Th}\) with independent sources only: zero the sources and combine—never solve equations.
  • \(V_{oc}\) and \(I_{sc}\): choose whichever is easier; \(R_{Th}=V_{oc}/I_{sc}\).
  • Load at maximum power? Immediately \(R_L=R_{Th}\) and \(P=V_{Th}^2/4R_{Th}\).
  • Only the load’s parameters matter; unplug it before computing \(V_{oc}\).

Example (40 s): bridge \(6,3,4,4\ \Omega\) with \(12\) V: \(V_{Th}=4-6=-2\) V, \(R_{Th}=2+2=4\ \Omega\), \(P_{max}=0.25\) W.

36.4 Time-constant shortcuts

  • \(\tau=R_{Th}C\) or \(L/R_{Th}\) with the new switch position and sources zeroed.
  • \(x(t)=x_\infty+(x_0-x_\infty)e^{-t/\tau}\): three numbers, one line.
  • Fraction done: \(1\tau\to63\%\), \(2\tau\to86\%\), \(3\tau\to95\%\), \(5\tau\to99\%\); half-way at \(0.69\tau\).
  • Energy dissipated in a full discharge \(=\) initial stored energy \(\tfrac12CV^2\).

36.5 Initial/final-value shortcuts

  • At \(t=\infty\): C open, L short. At \(t=0^+\): C is a source \(v_C(0^-)\), L a source \(i_L(0^-)\).
  • Laplace: \(x(0^+)=\lim_{s\to\infty}sX\), \(x(\infty)=\lim_{s\to0}sX\) (poles of \(sX\) in LHP). \(z\): \(x[0]=X(\infty)\), \(x[\infty]=\lim(1-z^{-1})X\).
  • DC gain of \(H(s)\) is \(H(0)\); of \(H(z)\) is \(H(1)\); of an FIR is \(\sum h\).

36.6 Pole–zero inspection

  • Stability: read poles; ignore zeros. CT: LHP; DT: inside unit circle.
  • Damping: \(s^2+2\zeta\omega_ns+\omega_n^2\): \(\omega_n=\sqrt{c}\), \(\zeta=b/2\omega_n\).
  • Filter type from the pole–zero plot: zero at \(z=1\) \(\to\) blocks DC (high-pass); zero at \(-1\) \(\to\) blocks Nyquist (low-pass); poles near \(e^{\pm j\Omega_0}\) \(\to\) band-pass.
  • Slope of the Bode plot: \(+20\) dB/dec per zero, \(-20\) per pole, counted up to the frequency of interest.

36.7 Convolution shortcuts

  • Support: start \(=\) sum of starts, end \(=\) sum of ends. Area: \(\int y=\int x\int h\). DT length \(L_x+L_h-1\).
  • \(x*\delta(t-t_0)=x(t-t_0)\); \(x*u=\) running integral; \(u*u=r\); rect\(*\)rect \(=\) triangle (peak \(=\) width).
  • DT: multiply polynomials (sequences as coefficients).
  • Check the answer with \(\sum y=\sum x\sum h\).

36.8 Fourier-transform properties

  • Never integrate if a pair plus a property does it: shift, modulation, scaling, differentiation, duality.
  • \(X(0)=\int x\); \(x(0)=\frac1{2\pi}\int X\); energy \(=\frac1{2\pi}\int|X|^2\).
  • Narrower in time \(\Rightarrow\) wider in frequency; real \(x\): \(|X|\) even; even \(x\): \(X\) real.
  • Product in time \(\Rightarrow\) bandwidths add; convolution \(\Rightarrow\) minimum.

36.9 Sampling shortcuts

  • Write \(f_m\) in Hz first; rate \(=2f_m\). Alias: \(|f-kf_s|\), choose \(k\) to land in \([0,f_s/2]\).
  • Bandpass: try \(k=\lfloor f_H/B\rfloor\) first, then check \(\frac{2f_H}k\le f_s\le\frac{2f_L}{k-1}\).
  • Reconstruct aliased signals by adding the aliased components at the same frequency (phases matter).

36.10 ROC shortcuts

  • Right-sided \(\to\) outside; left-sided \(\to\) inside; two-sided \(\to\) annulus; the ROC contains no poles.
  • Stable \(\Leftrightarrow\) unit circle (or \(j\omega\) axis) in the ROC; causal \(\Leftrightarrow\) exterior ROC.
  • A rational \(H(z)\) with poles at \(0.5\) and \(2\): three possible ROCs, exactly one is stable.

36.11 Filter-design shortcuts

  • Taps: \(N\approx\dfrac{c\pi}{\Delta\Omega}\) with \(c=1.8\) (rect), \(6.2\) (Hann), \(6.6\) (Hamming), \(11\) (Blackman); round up to an odd number for Type I.
  • Stopband requirement decides the window: \(21\) dB rect, \(44\) dB Hann, \(53\) dB Hamming, \(74\) dB Blackman.
  • Butterworth/Chebyshev order: compute \(\frac{10^{0.1\alpha_s}-1}{10^{0.1\alpha_p}-1}\) once, then \(N\); round up. Chebyshev order is always \(\le\) Butterworth’s.
  • Bilinear: prewarp \(\omega=\frac2T\tan\frac\Omega2\); pole map \(z=\frac{1+sT/2}{1-sT/2}\).
Speed rules

1) Read the whole question, note units (Hz vs rad/s, peak vs rms). 2) Decide the method within 15 s. 3) Skip anything not finished in 3 minutes (2-mark) or 90 s (1-mark). 4) For NAT, keep 3 significant digits. 5) Back-substitute or use a checksum (power balance, \(\sum y\), Parseval) on every numeric answer.
