Preface: How to Use This Book

chapterPreface: How to Use This Book

This book covers the Networks and Signals & Systems portions of the GATE Electronics & Communication Engineering syllabus. Together they carry roughly \(15\)–\(20\) marks out of 100, and they are the most scoring subjects: every question has a definite method, and once you know the method there is no ambiguity. The book runs from Ohm’s law to IIR filter design in seventeen parts.

Every chapter has the same anatomy, following the sequence

Concept \(\to\) Derivation \(\to\) Standard Results \(\to\) Solved Examples \(\to\) 1-Mark \(\to\) 2-Mark \(\to\) NAT \(\to\) Traps \(\to\) Chapter Test \(\to\) One-Page Revision

  • Concept and derivation. Formulas are derived once, so you can rebuild them under exam pressure rather than memorise them.
  • A figure for each case. Every circuit variant, waveform, spectrum or pole–zero pattern has its own drawing, produced from the actual mathematics (the plots are computed, not sketched by hand).
  • Boxes. Blue = formulae, orange = solved examples, green = GATE shortcuts, red = conceptual traps, grey = derivations, violet/indigo/teal = 1-mark, 2-mark, NAT problems, yellow = chapter test and one-page revision.
  • Answers follow every problem set, with the working shown in brief.
Important note on the “previous-year” problems

The problems in the 1-mark, 2-mark, NAT, MSQ and mock-test sections are original problems written to match the style, difficulty and recurring ideas of past GATE ECE papers. They are not verbatim copies of official questions, and the book does not attach a year or question number to any of them. Chapters 28 and 29 are organised as a topic-wise study map of what GATE repeatedly asks, each with worked problems of that type. For the exact official questions, download the papers from the GATE organising institute’s website and use this book as the solution toolkit.

Conventions. \(j=\sqrt{-1}\). Angular frequency \(\omega\) (rad/s) for continuous time, \(\Omega\) (rad/sample) for discrete time. \(u(t)\) and \(u[n]\) are unit steps; \(\delta(t)\) and \(\delta[n]\) unit impulses. Phasors use peak amplitude unless the text says RMS. Laplace transform means the unilateral transform unless the word “bilateral” appears. \(\sinc(x)=\sin(\pi x)/(\pi x)\) (the normalised sinc used in GATE and in most signal-processing texts). Where a problem says “assume ideal”, all sources and elements are ideal.

A suggested way to study. Read the derivation once, then close the book and re-derive the result on paper. Do the solved examples covering the solution first. Attempt the problem sets under a timer of about two minutes per mark. In the last week use the Formula Handbook (Chapter 34), the Conceptual Traps (Chapter 35), the Fast Techniques (Chapter 36) and the Mock Tests (Chapter 37).
